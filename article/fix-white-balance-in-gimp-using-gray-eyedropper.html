Fix White Balance in GIMP Using Gray Eyedropper

This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on how to correct color casts and balance photo tones in GIMP using the gray balance eyedropper tool. By targeting a neutral gray area within an image, GIMP automatically recalculates the red, green, and blue color channels to remove unwanted warm or cool tints. Follow the step-by-step process below to quickly achieve accurate, natural-looking colors in your photographs.

Step 1: Open Your Image in GIMP

Launch GIMP and open the image that requires color correction by navigating to File > Open, selecting your image file, and clicking Open.

Step 2: Open the Levels Tool

To access the gray eyedropper, open the Levels dialog by clicking Colors in the top menu and selecting Levels... from the dropdown list.

Step 3: Identify a Neutral Gray Area

Before using the tool, scan your image for a point that should be a neutral gray in real life. Ideal targets include:

  • Pavement, asphalt, or concrete.
  • Neutral gray clothing or fabric.
  • Gray stones or neutral shadows.
  • A photographic gray card (if one was included in the shoot).

Note: Avoid pure white highlights or deep black shadows, as selecting these with the gray eyedropper will severely distort the midtones.

Step 4: Select the Gray Balance Eyedropper

Inside the Levels dialog window, locate the three eyedropper icons situated near the bottom-right (below the Input Levels histogram):

  • The black eyedropper picks the black point.
  • The white eyedropper picks the white point.
  • The middle eyedropper—represented by a gray point—is the Pick gray point for all channels tool.

Click the middle (gray) eyedropper icon to activate it.

Step 5: Sample the Neutral Area

Move your cursor over the canvas and left-click directly on the neutral gray area you identified in Step 3. GIMP will instantly sample the color values at that pixel and adjust the red, green, and blue gamma values to make that point completely neutral, eliminating the ambient color cast across the entire image.

Step 6: Refine the Sample (Optional)

If the initial click leaves the image looking too warm, cool, or tinted:

  • Zoom in close to your neutral area using the Zoom Tool (Z) to avoid clicking on digital sensor noise or stray colored pixels.
  • Click another neutral spot nearby until the overall image appears balanced and true to life.

Step 7: Apply the Changes

Once you are satisfied with the corrected colors, click the OK button in the Levels dialog to permanently apply the white balance correction to your layer.
