GIMP Bucket Fill Diagonal Neighbors Explained

The Diagonal Neighbors option in GIMP modifies how the Bucket Fill tool detects connected pixels, expanding the algorithm from standard orthogonal evaluation to include diagonal connections. This article explains how enabling this setting alters pixel connectivity, how it changes the boundary limits of your fills, and the practical scenarios where it prevents or causes color bleeding across pixel art and line drawings.

Understanding Pixel Connectivity in GIMP

By default, GIMP’s Bucket Fill tool uses a 4-connected pixel method (orthogonal connectivity). When you click an area to fill it, GIMP evaluates adjacent pixels strictly in four directions: up, down, left, and right. If two similarly colored pixels only touch at their corners—sharing a vertex rather than an edge—the tool views them as separate entities and stops the fill at the boundary.

How Diagonal Neighbors Changes the Fill Area

Enabling the Diagonal neighbors checkbox switches the tool to an 8-connected pixel method. In this mode, GIMP searches in all eight directions surrounding a pixel: up, down, left, right, and all four diagonal corners.

This fundamentally alters the filled area in two ways:

  1. Bypasses Diagonal Barriers: In standard mode, a diagonal line of pixels (such as a 1-pixel-wide line sloping at 45 degrees) acts as an impenetrable barrier because the pixels only share corners. With Diagonal Neighbors enabled, the fill considers those diagonal points connected, allowing the color to spread along the diagonal path.
  2. Permits Color Leaks Through Gaps: Conversely, if your line art relies on corner-to-corner pixels to seal off an interior space, enabling Diagonal Neighbors will cause the fill color to pass directly through the diagonal gaps between the interior and exterior of your outline, flooding adjacent regions.

When to Use Diagonal Neighbors

  • Pixel Art Cleanup: When recoloring intricate pixel art, lines often consist of individual, corner-touching pixels. Enabling Diagonal Neighbors lets you select or fill an entire continuous diagonal stroke in a single click without manually filling each detached corner.
  • Segmented Patterns: If you are filling checkerboard or cross-hatched patterns where matching colors touch only at the vertices, this option allows the fill to spread across the entire grid seamlessly.

When to Keep It Disabled

Keep Diagonal Neighbors unchecked for standard line art coloring. Most hand-drawn digital sketches contain tiny diagonal gaps at corners. Leaving the setting off ensures the Bucket Fill treats strictly enclosed 4-way spaces as closed containers, preventing accidental color overflow into the rest of your canvas.
