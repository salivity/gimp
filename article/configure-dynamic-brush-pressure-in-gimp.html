Configure Dynamic Brush Pressure in GIMP

Configuring dynamic brush pressure in GIMP allows you to utilize the pressure sensitivity of your graphics tablet to control brush size, opacity, hardness, and other stroke characteristics. This guide covers the complete setup process, from enabling your hardware tablet drivers in GIMP's input settings to selecting presets and building custom paint dynamics mappings for a natural drawing workflow.

1. Enable Hardware Input Devices

Before GIMP can read stylus pressure, your tablet must be recognized as an active input device rather than a generic mouse.

  1. Open GIMP and navigate to Edit > Input Devices.
  2. Locate your graphics tablet stylus in the device list on the left (often labeled as stylus, pen, or the brand name of your tablet).
  3. Change the Mode dropdown from Disabled to Screen.
  4. Click Save and then Close.

2. Select a Brush and Access Tool Options

  1. Select the Paintbrush tool (or press P) from the Toolbox.
  2. Open the Tool Options dockable dialog (go to Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Tool Options if it is not visible).
  3. Scroll down to the Dynamics section.

3. Choose a Dynamic Preset

GIMP includes pre-configured dynamics that respond to stylus pressure immediately:

  • Click the dynamic preset icon (default is often Dynamics Off).
  • Choose a preset based on your needs:
    • Pressure Size: Changes brush thickness based on how hard you press.
    • Pressure Opacity: Adjusts ink transparency based on stylus force.
    • Pressure Variations: Modifies size, color, and opacity simultaneously.

4. Create and Customize Custom Dynamics

To map pressure to multiple specific parameters simultaneously:

  1. Open the Paint Dynamics panel via Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Paint Dynamics.
  2. Click the Create a new dynamics button at the bottom of the panel.
  3. In the Paint Dynamics Editor matrix, look at the Pressure row.
  4. Check the boxes for the properties you want pressure to affect:
    • Opacity: Lighter pressure yields a fainter stroke; harder pressure yields solid color.
    • Size: Lighter pressure draws thin lines; harder pressure draws thicker lines.
    • Hardness: Modifies edge sharpness depending on stylus force.
    • Force: Controls the amount of color deposited over time.

5. Fine-Tune Pressure Curves

For precise responsiveness, adjust the input curve:

  1. In the Paint Dynamics Editor, click the drop-down menu at the bottom labeled Mapping Matrix and select the specific parameter you want to tweak (such as Size or Opacity).
  2. Adjust the response curve by dragging points on the graph. A curved line helps adjust the sensitivity if your strokes feel too stiff or too sensitive.
  3. Test your brush strokes on the canvas to ensure the pressure transitions feel natural.
