How to Apply a Halftone Dot Filter in GIMP

Applying a halftone dot pattern filter to photos in GIMP is a simple way to create retro comic book, pop art, or vintage newspaper aesthetics. This guide covers the exact steps to convert any photo into a classic halftone dot image using GIMP's built-in Newsprint filter, including how to adjust dot size, angle, and color separation for the best results.

Step 1: Open and Prepare Your Image

  1. Launch GIMP and go to File > Open to load your image.
  2. Duplicate the base layer by right-clicking it in the Layers panel and selecting Duplicate Layer. This preserves your original image.
  3. For best results, increase the contrast of your photo before applying the effect. Go to Colors > Curves or Colors > Brightness-Contrast to make your darks darker and lights brighter, as the halftone effect relies heavily on tonal contrast to determine dot sizes.

Step 2: Choose Between Black-and-White or Color Halftone

  • For a classic black-and-white print look: Convert the layer to black and white by navigating to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate, choose your preferred mode, and click OK.
  • For a color CMYK comic print look: Keep your image in full color and proceed directly to the filter.

Step 3: Apply the Newsprint Filter

  1. Go to the top menu and select Filters > Distorts > Newsprint...
  2. In the Newsprint dialog box, ensure the Preview box is checked so you can view changes in real time.

Step 4: Configure the Halftone Settings

Adjust the following parameters in the Newsprint window to customize the pattern:

  • Pattern: Set this to Circle to generate standard round halftone dots. You can also experiment with Line or Diamond.
  • Period: This slider controls the size and spacing of the dots. Lower values produce smaller, finer dots (better for subtle details), while higher values produce large, bold dots (ideal for a pop-art look).
  • Colorspace:
    • Select RGB or CMYK if you want individual overlapping color dots.
    • Select Greyscale if you are creating a monochromatic print.
  • Angle: Adjust the screen angle for each color channel to prevent unintended moiré patterns. Default values usually work well, but you can tweak them to change the visual flow of the pattern.
  • Oversampling: Increase this value to smooth out rough pixel edges on the dots for a cleaner, high-resolution finish.

Step 5: Finalize and Export

  1. Click OK to apply the filter to your layer.
  2. (Optional) Lower the opacity of the halftone layer or set its blend mode to Multiply or Overlay if you want to blend the halftone texture over the original photograph.
  3. Go to File > Export As..., choose your preferred file format (such as PNG or JPEG), and click Export.
