How to Retouch Skin in GIMP While Keeping Texture

This guide covers how to remove wrinkles and blemishes in GIMP without creating an unnatural, overly airbrushed look by using the frequency separation method. Frequency separation splits your photograph into two distinct layers: a low-frequency layer containing colors, shadows, and skin tones, and a high-frequency layer containing fine details like pores and hair. By separating these elements, you can smooth out uneven tones and wrinkle shadows while preserving natural skin texture.

Step 1: Duplicate Your Base Layer

  1. Open your portrait in GIMP.
  2. Go to the Layers panel and duplicate the background layer twice.
  3. Rename the middle layer to Low Frequency (Tone/Color).
  4. Rename the top layer to High Frequency (Texture).
  5. Hide the High Frequency layer by clicking the eye icon next to it.

Step 2: Blur the Low Frequency Layer

  1. Select the Low Frequency layer.
  2. Navigate to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur.
  3. Adjust the Size X and Size Y sliders until the fine skin textures (pores, fine lines) blur away, but major features (eyes, lips, facial shape) remain recognizable.
  4. Click OK.

Step 3: Extract the High Frequency Layer

  1. Make the High Frequency layer visible again and select it.
  2. Set the layer mode of the High Frequency layer to Grain Extract.
  3. Right-click the High Frequency layer in the Layers panel and choose New from Visible.
  4. Rename this new resulting layer to Texture Final.
  5. Delete the original High Frequency layer.
  6. Change the layer mode of Texture Final to Grain Merge.
  7. Your image will now appear completely normal and identical to the original, but the texture and color are split between the two layers.

Step 4: Remove Tone Discoloration and Deep Shadows

  1. Select the Low Frequency layer.
  2. Select the Healing Tool (H) or the Smudge Tool (S) from the toolbox.
  3. Ensure the tool's source is set to Current Layer.
  4. Gently blend uneven skin tones, discoloration, under-eye bags, and the dark shadows caused by deep wrinkles. Because texture is on the upper layer, you can blend freely without blurring the skin pores.

Step 5: Remove Blemishes and Fine Lines

  1. Select the Texture Final layer.
  2. Select the Clone Tool (C) or Healing Tool (H).
  3. Set the tool mode to Current Layer.
  4. Hold Ctrl (or Cmd on macOS) and click on a clean area of skin to sample its texture.
  5. Paint over pimples, scars, and sharp wrinkle lines to replace damaged texture with clean texture.

Step 6: Adjust Opacity for a Natural Finish

  1. If the skin texture appears too sharp or unnatural, lower the opacity of the Texture Final layer slightly (between 85% and 95%).
  2. Toggle the visibility of both edited layers on and off to compare your retouched portrait with the original image.
