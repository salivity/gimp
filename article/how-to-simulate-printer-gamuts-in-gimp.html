How to Simulate Printer Gamuts in GIMP

Simulating a printer gamut directly on your monitor—a process known as soft proofing—allows you to preview how colors will shift when printed onto specific paper and ink sets. This guide explains how to configure GIMP’s color management settings and display filters using target ICC printer profiles. By following these steps, you will learn how to set up soft proofing, toggle printer simulations, and identify out-of-gamut colors before sending files to the press.

1. Acquire and Install the Printer ICC Profile

Before configuring GIMP, you must have the specific ICC profile for your target printer, ink, and paper combination. Printer manufacturers and print labs typically provide these profiles on their websites. Once downloaded, install the profile to your operating system's default color directory, or place it in a dedicated folder on your computer so GIMP can access it.

2. Configure Color Management in Preferences

To establish the baseline profile for your printer simulation, set up GIMP's global color management preferences:

  1. Open GIMP and navigate to Edit > Preferences (or GIMP > Preferences on macOS).
  2. In the left panel, click on Color Management.
  3. Set Image color management to Color managed display.
  4. In the Color proofing profiles section, locate the Soft-proofing profile field. Click the dropdown menu, select Select color profile from disk..., and browse to your downloaded printer ICC profile.
  5. Set the Soft-proofing rendering intent according to your workflow:
    • Perceptual: Preserves visual relationships between colors, ideal for general photography.
    • Relative Colorimetric: Retains all in-gamut colors exactly and maps out-of-gamut colors to the closest printable alternative, standard for high-accuracy commercial printing.
  6. Check Mark out of gamut colors if you want GIMP to highlight colors your printer cannot reproduce.
  7. Click OK to save the settings.

3. Apply the Display Filter via View Menu

Once the profile is defined in Preferences, you can toggle the printer simulation directly on your active canvas:

  1. Open the image you want to evaluate.
  2. Navigate to View > Color Management.
  3. Select Soft-Proofing to activate the printer gamut simulation.

Your screen will adjust to reflect the dynamic range, contrast, and color limits of the selected paper and printer profile. To see which specific colors cannot be printed accurately, go back to View > Color Management and select Out of Gamut Warning. Unprintable pixels will be overlaid with a highlight color (typically magenta or black, depending on your configuration).

4. Alternative Method: Using the Display Filters Dialog

If you prefer applying the simulation via the dedicated display filter stack rather than the global view settings:

  1. Navigate to View > Display Filters...
  2. In the Available Filters list on the left, select Color Management and click the right arrow to move it to the Active Filters column.
  3. Select Color Management under the active list to display its properties at the bottom of the window.
  4. Set the Filter profile option to your target printer ICC profile.
  5. Choose your desired Rendering intent and decide whether to enable the Out of gamut display checkbox.
  6. Click OK.

You can adjust contrast, levels, and saturation layers beneath this filter until the soft proof matches your desired visual outcome within the printer's physical limitations.
