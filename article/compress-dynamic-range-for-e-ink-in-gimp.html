Compress Dynamic Range for E-Ink in GIMP

Electronic paper (e-ink) displays feature significantly lower contrast ratios and limited grayscale palettes compared to standard LCD or OLED panels. This guide details the exact settings in GIMP—primarily within the Levels, Curves, and Indexed Color tools—required to compress and remap an image's tonal dynamic range. By following these steps, you will prevent shadow crushing, eliminate washed-out highlights, and maximize visibility on low-contrast e-ink screens.

1. Convert to Grayscale

Before adjusting tonal ranges, remove color data to evaluate contrast accurately:

  • Navigate to Image > Mode > Grayscale.
  • Alternatively, use Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate and choose Luminance to preserve perceptual brightness based on human vision.

2. Compress Dynamic Range with the Levels Tool

The Levels tool is the most direct way to compress dynamic range by constraining the absolute white and black boundaries of an image.

  • Open the tool via Colors > Levels.
  • Locate the Output Levels section at the bottom of the dialog (this features a gradient bar with two sliders: Black at 0 and White at 255).
  • Lift the Black Point: Drag the black Output slider to the right (typically between 15 and 35). This prevents deep shadows from pooling into muddy, unreadable blacks on e-ink panels.
  • Lower the White Point: Drag the white Output slider to the left (typically between 220 and 240). This pulls bright highlights down to a range the reflective display can render without clipping.
  • Adjust Midtone Gamma: Under Input Levels, move the middle gray slider. Setting this between 1.10 and 1.30 brightens midtones, which helps prevent medium grays from dropping into the compressed dark regions.

3. Fine-Tune Transitions with the Curves Tool

If an image requires selective tonal compression rather than a uniform squeeze, use the Curves tool.

  • Open Colors > Curves.
  • Lift Shadows: Click the bottom-left anchor point (representing pure black) and move it upward along the vertical axis to an input/output value of roughly 0 / 20.
  • Tame Highlights: Click the top-right anchor point (pure white) and drag it downward along the vertical axis to roughly 255 / 235.
  • Flatten the Curve Ends: Create a slight reverse-S curve by gently pulling down the upper-midtones and pushing up the lower-midtones to compress contrast further, keeping image details centered in the panel’s most legible gray band.

4. Apply Spatial Dithering via Indexed Color

Most e-ink screens only support 1-bit (black and white) or 4-bit (16 levels of gray) rendering. Once the tonal range is compressed, quantize the image:

  • Navigate to Image > Mode > Indexed.
  • Select Generate optimum palette and set the number of colors to 16 (or 4, depending on your specific display's hardware support).
  • Set Color dithering to Floyd-Steinberg (normal) or Floyd-Steinberg (reduced color bleeding). Dithering breaks up the compressed tones into micro-patterns that eliminate harsh banding artifacts across the low-contrast medium.
