Divide Blend Mode for Illumination Correction in GIMP

The Divide blend mode in GIMP is a specialized tool used to normalize uneven lighting, remove severe vignetting, and extract flat backgrounds from improperly lit images. By dividing the color values of the original image by an isolated map of the light falloff, this blend mode mathematically cancels out lighting gradients and restores consistent exposure across the entire canvas.

The Mathematical Principle

In digital imaging, brightness is a product of illumination and surface reflectance:

\[\text{Image Brightness} = \text{Illumination} \times \text{Reflectance}\]

Because illumination acts as a multiplier on the underlying subject, standard additive modes (like Screen or Add) or subtractive adjustments cannot cleanly neutralize uneven lighting without clipping highlights or muddying shadows.

The Divide blend mode applies the following formula pixel-by-pixel:

\[\text{Result} = \left(\frac{\text{Base Layer}}{\text{Blend Layer}}\right) \times 256\]

When the blend layer contains an accurate representation of the lighting variations (the illumination map), dividing the base image by this layer cancels the illumination component, leaving only the uniform reflectance of the subject.

How Divide Mode Corrects Illumination

To use Divide for illumination correction in GIMP, the general workflow relies on creating a background model of the lighting:

  1. Creating the Illumination Map: A duplicate of the original image is heavily blurred using filters such as Gaussian Blur or Wavelet Decompose until all fine subject details disappear, leaving only the broad gradients of light and shadow.
  2. Applying Divide Mode: The blurred layer is placed above the original image, and its blend mode is set to Divide.
  3. Neutralizing the Gradients: Bright areas in the blend layer divide corresponding bright areas in the base image, while dark areas divide dark areas. This scales every region of the image proportionally to a neutral baseline, eliminating hot spots and dark corners.
  4. Tonal Readjustment: Because division often shifts the overall luminance toward pure white, a Levels or Curves adjustment layer is placed on top to restore natural contrast and black levels.

Key Use Cases

  • Document Digitization: Removing shadows cast by phones or uneven desk lamps when digitizing paper documents, yielding a uniform white background.
  • Astrophotography (Synthetic Flat-Fielding): Eliminating telescope vignetting, dust donuts, and light pollution gradients from deep-sky captures.
  • Macro and Studio Photography: Correcting uneven light falloff across smooth backdrops without affecting the edge detail of the subject.
  • Microscopy: Correcting uneven field illumination (Kohler illumination imperfections) across specimen slides.
