What Is the Clothify Filter in GIMP?

The Clothify filter in GIMP is a built-in artistic tool designed to give any image, graphic, or layer the tactile appearance of woven fabric, canvas, or cloth. This guide explains how the Clothify filter works, breaks down its adjustable parameters, and outlines its practical applications in photo editing and digital design.

How the Clothify Filter Works

The Clothify filter functions as an automated script (Script-Fu) that generates a custom cloth-like texture and applies it directly to the active layer.

Under the hood, the filter automatically creates a duplicate grayscale layer, generates procedural noise, applies directional blurs, and then uses GIMP's bump map engine to project that woven pattern onto your original image. The final result simulates light and shadow catching the individual threads of a woven material.

To access the tool in GIMP, navigate to Filters > Artistic > Clothify... from the top menu.

Key Settings and Parameters

When you open the Clothify dialog box, you can fine-tune several settings to change the weave type and fabric weight:

  • Blur X and Blur Y: These sliders control the horizontal (X) and vertical (Y) blur applied to the generated noise. Adjusting these values determines the thickness, stretch, and smoothness of the fabric threads. Higher values create wider, softer threads, while lower values produce a tighter, finer weave.
  • Azimuth: This parameter controls the directional angle of the light source in degrees (0 to 360). Changing the azimuth alters where shadows fall across the weave, affecting the direction from which the fabric appears illuminated.
  • Elevation: This setting dictates the height or angle of the light source relative to the image surface (0.5 to 90 degrees). A lower elevation casts longer, sharper shadows that make the texture look deeper, whereas a higher elevation provides flatter, more subtle lighting.
  • Depth: This slider controls the intensity of the bump map effect. Lower depth values produce delicate textures resembling fine linen or paper, while higher values create rugged, deep weaves similar to burlap, canvas, or denim.

Common Uses for Clothify

  • Simulating Canvas Art: Applying Clothify to digital paintings or photographs produces the authentic appearance of oil on canvas.
  • Textile and Fashion Mockups: Designers can convert flat color blocks and patterns into realistic fabric swatches.
  • Adding Tactile Backgrounds: The filter can transform plain backgrounds into textured paper, rough cloth, or artistic backdrops for typography.
