Create Beveled Glass Text in GIMP

This tutorial provides a complete walkthrough for simulating realistic beveled glass typography in GIMP using offset highlights, inner shadows, and layer blend modes. By creating directional light reflections and soft depth cues over a textured background, you can achieve a transparent, refractive glass effect without relying on third-party plugins.

Step 1: Canvas and Background Setup

  1. Create a new image in GIMP (File > New) with your desired dimensions.
  2. Paste a colorful or textured background onto the canvas (such as a photograph or gradient). Glass is transparent, so the effect relies entirely on distorting and highlighting the background beneath it.

Step 2: Generate the Typography Mask

  1. Select the Text Tool (T) and choose a heavy, bold font (e.g., Montserrat Black, Arial Black) with white color. Thick letterforms provide enough surface area for bevels and refractions.
  2. Type your text, position it in the center, and right-click the text layer in the Layers panel.
  3. Choose Alpha to Selection to load the outline of the text.
  4. Create a new transparent layer directly above the background and name it Glass Base.
  5. With the selection active, fill it with white using the Bucket Fill Tool (Shift + B), then set the layer's Opacity to approximately 10% to 15%. Deselect everything via Select > None (Ctrl + Shift + A).

Step 3: Create the Offset Highlight

The highlight simulates the top-left edge of the glass catching a light source.

  1. Right-click the original text layer and select Alpha to Selection.
  2. Create a new transparent layer named Highlight.
  3. Fill the active selection with pure white (#FFFFFF).
  4. Keep the selection active. Use the Move Tool (M) to shift the selection area down and to the right by 2 to 4 pixels (hold Alt while using the arrow keys to nudge only the selection boundary).
  5. Press Delete on your keyboard. This leaves a thin white rim along the top and left edges of the characters.
  6. Clear the selection (Select > None).
  7. Apply a subtle blur: Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur, set both the Size X and Y to 1.5 or 2.0 pixels, and click OK.
  8. Set the Highlight layer mode to Addition or Screen, and lower the opacity to around 70% to soften the shine.

Step 4: Create the Inner Shadow

The shadow creates physical thickness by simulating the underside and opposite edges of the glass.

  1. Right-click the original text layer and select Alpha to Selection.
  2. Create a new transparent layer named Inner Shadow.
  3. Fill the selection with pure black (#000000).
  4. Move the selection area up and to the left by 2 to 4 pixels using the arrow keys.
  5. Press Delete, leaving a black crescent along the bottom and right edges of the characters.
  6. Clear the selection (Select > None).
  7. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur, set the blur radius to 3.0 pixels, and apply it.
  8. Right-click the original text layer, choose Alpha to Selection, invert the selection (Select > Invert or Ctrl + I), click on the Inner Shadow layer, and press Delete. This cleans up any shadow blur spilling outside the text boundary.
  9. Deselect all (Select > None) and change the Inner Shadow layer mode to Multiply, setting its opacity between 40% and 60%.

Step 5: Add the Inner Edge Refraction

  1. Right-click the original text layer and choose Alpha to Selection.
  2. Create a new transparent layer named Inner Rim.
  3. Go to Select > Shrink and shrink the selection by 2 or 3 pixels.
  4. Go to Select > Invert, then fill the resulting thin perimeter with white.
  5. Invert the selection again, press Delete, and deselect all.
  6. Apply a 1.0 pixel Gaussian Blur to the Inner Rim layer and set its layer mode to Overlay at 50% opacity to give the glass a defined, polished border.

Step 6: Final Adjustments

Hide the visibility of the original solid text layer by clicking its eye icon. The composition should now consist solely of your background, the faint Glass Base, the soft Inner Shadow, the sharp Highlight, and the Inner Rim. Tweak the opacity of the highlight and shadow layers to match the intensity of the lighting in your specific background image.
