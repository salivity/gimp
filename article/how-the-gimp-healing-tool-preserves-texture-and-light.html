How the GIMP Healing Tool Preserves Texture and Light

GIMP’s Healing tool achieves seamless retouching through an advanced mathematical blending technique known as Poisson image editing. Unlike standard copy-and-paste tools, it analyzes the boundary values of the destination area and merges them with the fine details of the source sample. This allows GIMP to transfer source patterns and textures while automatically conforming to the local lighting, color tone, and curvature of the target surface.

The Limitation of Direct Pixel Copying

To understand the Healing tool, it helps to contrast it with the standard Clone tool. The Clone tool performs an absolute pixel-for-pixel transfer. If you sample an area with flat, bright lighting and stamp it into a shaded area, the Clone tool copies the flat brightness directly over the shadow, resulting in harsh, visible edges and an unnatural finish.

The Healing tool avoids this by treating color and lighting not as fixed values, but as smooth gradients that can be recalculated.

The Underlying Math: Poisson Image Editing

At the core of the GIMP Healing algorithm is the Poisson equation, a partial differential equation used extensively in computer graphics for seamless image cloning. Instead of copying raw RGB values, the tool calculates the Laplacian (the rate of change between adjacent pixels) of the source sample and applies it across the target area using Dirichlet boundary conditions.

In practice, this process splits the image data into two distinct components:

  • High-Frequency Details (Texture and Gradients): The sharp variations in tone—such as skin pores, fabric weaves, or surface scratches—represent the local gradient field. The Healing tool samples this structural information from the source.
  • Low-Frequency Values (Color and Illumination): The broad washes of color, ambient light, and shadow represent the baseline brightness. The tool reads these values from the boundary surrounding your brush stroke in the destination area.

How the Blending Occurs

When you apply a brush stroke with the Healing tool, the software executes a multi-step calculation:

  1. Boundary Sampling: The algorithm inspects the pixels immediately surrounding the painted destination area to determine the local brightness, tint, and shadow profile.
  2. Gradient Mapping: It extracts the directional contrast and pattern details from your chosen source point.
  3. Linear Equation Solving: The algorithm solves a Poisson system of linear equations. It forces the interior pixels to retain the texture variations of the source while mathematically stretching and shifting their absolute colors so that the borders match the destination perfectly.

Because the boundary conditions are derived strictly from the destination image, the overall luminance and underlying lighting transitions are preserved. Meanwhile, because the internal variations follow the source data, the desired pattern or texture is cleanly rendered across the surface without hard seams or mismatched exposure.
