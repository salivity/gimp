How to Create a Tilt-Shift Effect in GIMP

Simulating a tilt-shift effect transforms real-world scenes into optical illusions that resemble miniature scale models. This tutorial explains how to achieve this popular visual style in GIMP by applying selective depth-of-field blurring, layer masks, and color enhancements.

Step 1: Select and Open Your Image

For the best results, choose a photo taken from a high angle or elevated perspective looking down at a scene, such as a city street, train yard, or harbor. Open your image in GIMP by navigating to File > Open.

Step 2: Duplicate the Background Layer

Preserve your original image and set up the blur layer:

  1. Locate the Layers panel on the right side of the workspace.
  2. Right-click the base image layer and select Duplicate Layer (or press Shift + Ctrl + D).
  3. Rename this top layer to "Blur" for easy reference.

Step 3: Apply a Gaussian Blur

  1. Make sure the "Blur" layer is selected.
  2. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur... in the top menu.
  3. Adjust the Size X and Size Y sliders to increase the blur amount. A value between 15 and 30 pixels generally works well, depending on your image resolution.
  4. Click OK to apply the effect.

Step 4: Add a Layer Mask

  1. Right-click the "Blur" layer in the Layers panel.
  2. Select Add Layer Mask...
  3. Choose White (full opacity) and click Add.

Step 5: Reveal the Focal Plane with the Gradient Tool

  1. Select the Gradient Tool from the Toolbox (or press G).
  2. In the Tool Options dock, set the gradient to FG to BG (RGB) and ensure your foreground color is black and background color is white.
  3. Set the gradient Shape to Bilinear.
  4. Click and drag the gradient across your main point of interest on the canvas. Dragging outward from your subject will paint black onto the layer mask, rendering that central strip sharp and in-focus while leaving the top and bottom areas smoothly blurred.

Step 6: Boost Contrast and Saturation

Miniatures and toys typically have higher color saturation and sharper contrast than real-world scenes.

  1. Click the base image layer in the Layers panel.
  2. Go to Colors > Curves... to create a slight "S-curve" that deepens shadows and brightens highlights, then click OK.
  3. Go to Colors > Hue-Saturation... and increase the Saturation slider by 15 to 30% to give the scene a bright, toy-like appearance.

Step 7: Export the Final Image

Once satisfied with the focal depth and color balance, go to File > Export As..., choose your preferred file format (such as JPEG or PNG), and click Export.
