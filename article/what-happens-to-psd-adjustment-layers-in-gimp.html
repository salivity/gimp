What Happens to PSD Adjustment Layers in GIMP?

When you import an Adobe Photoshop PSD file containing adjustment layers into GIMP, the non-destructive adjustment layers are not preserved as editable elements. Because GIMP lacks native support for Photoshop's proprietary adjustment layer system, it either ignores these layers entirely, converts them into unsupported blank layers, or relies on a flattened preview image. Consequently, the individual dynamic effects—such as Curves, Levels, or Hue/Saturation adjustments—cannot be modified within GIMP.

Lack of Native Adjustment Layer Support

GIMP processes raster graphics differently from Photoshop. While Photoshop applies adjustment layers dynamically over underlying layers without altering their raw pixel data, GIMP operates primarily through direct pixel manipulation on standard layers. When GIMP parses the PSD file format, it does not possess the internal architecture to interpret Photoshop’s mathematical instructions for live layer adjustments. As a result, the live connection between the adjustment layer and the image data below it is severed upon import.

How GIMP Renders the File

How the image visually appears in GIMP depends heavily on how the PSD was saved in Photoshop:

  • With "Maximize Compatibility" Enabled: Photoshop saves a pre-rendered, flattened composite of the entire project inside the PSD file. GIMP can read this cached visual data, meaning the final image may appear correct visually as a whole, but individual adjustment layers will still be missing or stripped of their editing functionality.
  • Without "Maximize Compatibility": GIMP attempts to reconstruct the image solely from individual layer data. Because it cannot execute the adjustment layer instructions, it simply skips them. This often results in a dramatically different visual appearance compared to the original design, as colors, contrasts, and tonal values revert to their raw states.

Common Workarounds

To ensure your design survives the transition from Photoshop to GIMP, consider the following solutions:

  • Merge Visible Layers in Photoshop: Before exporting the file, merge the adjustment layers with their target pixel layers (or duplicate and merge them). This bakes the adjustments directly into the pixels, preserving the visual look in GIMP at the cost of future adjustability.
  • Export as High-Quality Flat Files: If multi-layer editing is not required, export the project from Photoshop as a high-bitrate TIFF or uncompressed PNG to retain exact color fidelity.
  • Recreate Adjustments in GIMP: If editable adjustments are necessary, import the raw pixel layers into GIMP and manually reapply the effects using GIMP’s native color tools found under the Colors menu, such as Curves, Levels, and Exposure.
