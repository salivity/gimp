GIMP Rotate 90° Clockwise Image Command

The "Rotate 90° Clockwise" command in GIMP is a global transform tool that reorients an entire image project a quarter-turn to the right. This article explains how the command alters the canvas, its impact on all project layers and elements, and how it differs from single-layer transformations to help you manage document orientation efficiently.

To access this command, navigate to the top menu and select Image > Transform > Rotate 90° clockwise.

When executed at the document level, the command's primary function is to flip the canvas dimensions while shifting all graphical data 90 degrees to the right. If your original image is 1920 pixels wide by 1080 pixels high, the rotated document instantly becomes 1080 pixels wide by 1920 pixels high. This automatic dimension swap ensures that your visual content remains completely within the bounds of the canvas, preventing any clipping or unwanted cropping.

Applying this command to the whole image affects every single asset within the file simultaneously. Unlike the layer-specific rotate tools, the document-level rotation applies to:

  • All Layers: Every visible, hidden, or locked layer is rotated around the center of the canvas in unison, preserving their exact spatial relationships to one another.
  • Layer Masks and Channels: Any alpha channels, layer masks, and selection channels are rotated alongside their respective image data.
  • Paths and Guides: Vector paths and user-placed reference guides shift automatically to match the new orientation.

Because 90 degrees is an orthogonal angle, the operation transforms the pixel grid without requiring complex resampling or interpolation. This means the rotation is virtually lossless, keeping your edges sharp and preventing the blurriness often caused by arbitrary, free-angle rotations.

The primary use case for this command is correcting sideways photographs—frequently seen in portrait photos imported without proper EXIF orientation metadata—or repurposing landscape graphics into portrait layouts before beginning detailed composition work.
