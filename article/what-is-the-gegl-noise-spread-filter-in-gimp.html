What Is the GEGL Noise Spread Filter in GIMP?

The GEGL Noise Spread filter in GIMP is a distortion tool designed to scatter existing pixels randomly across a defined area to produce textured, diffused, or painterly visual effects. This article explains the primary purpose of the Noise Spread filter, how its parameters manipulate pixel data, and the most practical applications for its use in photo editing and graphic design.

How the Noise Spread Filter Works

Unlike standard noise filters that generate new random colored or monochrome pixels (such as RGB Noise), the GEGL Noise Spread filter displaces the pixels that are already present in the layer or selection. It shifts each pixel within a user-defined horizontal and vertical radius according to a random algorithm. The original colors, tones, and image details are preserved, but their spatial arrangement becomes randomized.

Key Filter Parameters

  • Amount X: Controls the maximum horizontal distance (in pixels) that pixels can be displaced to the left or right.
  • Amount Y: Controls the maximum vertical distance (in pixels) that pixels can be displaced up or down.
  • Seed: Sets the base value for the pseudo-random number generator, allowing users to cycle through different randomized distribution patterns while keeping the same spread values.

Common Creative and Practical Applications

1. Simulating Frosted Glass and Diffusion

By applying a low-to-medium spread evenly across the X and Y axes, the filter creates the optical effect of looking through textured, frosted, or patterned glass.

2. Creating Painterly and Media Textures

When applied to illustrations, digital paintings, or photographic subjects, the filter breaks up smooth gradients and sharp lines. This mimics the natural bleeding and rough texture of traditional media such as pastels, watercolors, or chalk on rough paper.

3. Directional Motion and Smear Artifacts

Setting one axis higher than the other (for example, a high Amount X with an Amount Y of zero) produces a directional streaking effect. This can be used for motion simulation, chromatic-style distortion, or digital glitch art.

4. Edge Softening and Seamless Blending

Noise Spread can break up rigid, vector-sharp edges on cutouts or masks, making it easier to blend composited elements naturally into a background with film grain or atmospheric noise.
