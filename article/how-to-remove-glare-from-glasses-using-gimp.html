How to Remove Glare from Glasses Using GIMP

Removing glare and reflections from eyeglasses is an essential skill for retouching portrait photography. This guide provides a straightforward method to eliminate glass reflections in GIMP using non-destructive editing techniques, primarily leveraging the Heal Tool, the Clone Tool, and targeted color correction layers to restore hidden eye details and natural skin tones.

Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

  1. Open your portrait in GIMP.
  2. In the Layers panel on the right, right-click your background image layer and select Duplicate Layer (or press Shift + Ctrl + D).
  3. Rename this new layer to "Glare Removal." Working on a duplicate ensures your original image remains untouched.

Step 2: Sample and Heal Minor Glare

For faint glare, soft highlights, or reflections over smooth skin areas:

  1. Select the Heal Tool from the Toolbox (keyboard shortcut: H).
  2. In the Tool Options, choose a soft-edged brush (such as 2. Hardness 050) and adjust the size so it is slightly larger than the reflection spot.
  3. Hold Ctrl (or Cmd on macOS) and click on an adjacent, glare-free area of skin or the eye to set the source reference point.
  4. Release Ctrl and gently paint over the glare. The Heal Tool blends the texture of the source area with the color and brightness of the target area.

Step 3: Reconstruct Eyes with the Clone Tool

If the glare covers critical details like the iris, pupil, or eyelashes:

  1. Select the Clone Tool (keyboard shortcut: C).
  2. If only one lens has severe glare, set your source point by holding Ctrl and clicking on the unobstructed eye. If cloning from the opposite eye, you may need to mirror the brush or work in small, precise increments.
  3. Lower the Opacity of the Clone Tool to roughly 30% to 50% in the Tool Options.
  4. Paint slowly over the affected area to gradually rebuild the structure of the iris, eyelid, and pupil without creating harsh, unnatural edges.

Step 4: Fix Discoloration and Haze

Strong glare often leaves behind washed-out, white, or green tinting:

  1. Select the Free Select Tool (Lasso) and draw a loose selection around the remaining washed-out area inside the glasses frame.
  2. Go to Select > Feather... and set the radius to 5–10 pixels to soften the selection edges.
  3. Go to Colors > Curves... or Colors > Levels....
  4. Lower the midtones and highlights slightly to bring back contrast and match the luminance of the surrounding eye area.
  5. If a color cast remains, go to Colors > Color Temperature... or Colors > Hue-Chroma... to neutralize unwanted green or blue reflection tones.

Step 5: Final Blending

  1. Zoom out to 100% view to evaluate the portrait as a whole.
  2. If the corrections look too intense or painted-on, lower the Opacity slider of the "Glare Removal" layer slightly in the Layers panel until the result blends naturally with the original image.
