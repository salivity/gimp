Color Balance in GIMP: Shadows, Midtones, Highlights

Adjusting color balance across shadows, midtones, and highlights independently is essential for correcting color casts and achieving professional color grading in GIMP. By using GIMP's dedicated Color Balance tool or the Curves tool, you can isolate specific tonal ranges of your image to shift individual color spectrums without degrading the overall exposure or altering unaffected ranges.

Using the Color Balance Tool

  1. Open the Tool: Open your image in GIMP, then navigate to the top menu and select Colors > Color Balance...
  2. Select the Tonal Range: At the top of the dialog box, under Select Range to Adjust, choose one of the three options:
    • Shadows: Affects only the darkest areas of the image.
    • Midtones: Affects the neutral, mid-exposure areas.
    • Highlights: Affects the brightest areas of the image.
  3. Adjust the Color Sliders: Move the sliders toward the color you want to introduce into the selected tonal range:
    • Cyan / Red
    • Magenta / Green
    • Yellow / Blue
  4. Repeat for Other Ranges: Switch the radio button to the next tonal range (e.g., from Shadows to Highlights) and adjust the sliders accordingly.
  5. Preserve Luminosity: Ensure the Preserve luminosity checkbox is checked at the bottom of the window. This prevents the brightness levels of your image from changing as you shift the colors.
  6. Apply Changes: Click OK to apply the color adjustments to your active layer.

Advanced Control with the Curves Tool

For finer precision across tonal ranges:

  1. Go to Colors > Curves...
  2. In the Channel dropdown, switch from Value to a specific color channel (Red, Green, or Blue).
  3. Click and drag the curve:
    • Bottom-left section: Modifies the Shadows.
    • Center section: Modifies the Midtones.
    • Top-right section: Modifies the Highlights.
  4. Dragging a point upward adds that channel's primary color; dragging downward adds its complementary color.
  5. Click OK to confirm your adjustments.
