How to Use the Color Accent Technique in GIMP

The color accent technique, commonly known as selective color or the color splash effect, emphasizes a specific subject by retaining its natural hue while converting the surrounding background to black and white. In GIMP, achieving this effect is straightforward and non-destructive using duplicate layers and layer masks. This step-by-step guide explains how to isolate a single color against a monochrome background with precision.

Step 1: Open Your Image and Duplicate the Layer

  1. Launch GIMP and open your photo by going to File > Open.
  2. Open the Layers dialog panel (Ctrl + L on Windows/Linux or Cmd + L on macOS) if it is not already visible.
  3. Right-click the base layer and select Duplicate Layer (or press Shift + Ctrl + D).
  4. Rename the top layer to "Monochrome" and keep the bottom layer as "Original Color" to stay organized.

Step 2: Desaturate the Top Layer

  1. Click on the top "Monochrome" layer to select it.
  2. Navigate to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate... in the top menu.
  3. In the dialog box, choose your preferred conversion mode (such as Luminance or Luma) depending on which provides the best contrast for your photo.
  4. Click OK to apply the monochrome effect to the top layer.

Step 3: Add a Layer Mask

  1. Right-click the "Monochrome" layer in the Layers panel and select Add Layer Mask...
  2. In the options window, select White (full opacity) and click Add.
  3. A white rectangle will appear next to the layer thumbnail, indicating that the mask is active.

Step 4: Paint to Reveal the Accent Color

  1. Select the Paintbrush Tool (P) from the Toolbox.
  2. Set the foreground color to pure Black (#000000).
  3. Choose a soft-edged brush (such as Hardness 050) to ensure smooth blending along the edges of your subject.
  4. Paint over the areas of the image where you want the original color to show through. Painting with black creates transparency on the monochrome layer, exposing the vibrant color layer underneath.
  5. If you make a mistake and reveal color outside your intended area, switch the foreground color to pure White (#FFFFFF) and paint over the mistake to restore the monochrome background.

Step 5: Refine and Enhance the Color

  1. Zoom in (+ key) to clean up fine edges around the colored object using a smaller brush size.
  2. To make the isolated color stand out even more, select the bottom "Original Color" layer and go to Colors > Saturation... to slightly increase the color intensity.
  3. Once satisfied with the result, navigate to File > Export As... to save your final image in your desired format (e.g., JPEG or PNG).
