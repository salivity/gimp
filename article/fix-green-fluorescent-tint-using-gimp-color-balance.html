Fix Green Fluorescent Tint Using GIMP Color Balance

Fluorescent indoor lighting often produces an unnatural, sickly greenish tint across interior photographs. This guide provides a straightforward method for neutralizing that green color cast using the Color Balance tool in GIMP. By targeting the shadows, midtones, and highlights individually and introducing magenta—the complementary color to green—you can restore natural skin tones and accurate interior colors within minutes.

Step 1: Duplicate Your Base Layer

Open your photograph in GIMP. Before making any color adjustments, create a duplicate of your original image layer by pressing Shift + Ctrl + D (or right-clicking the layer in the Layers panel and choosing Duplicate Layer). Working on a duplicate ensures your original image remains untouched and allows you to toggle visibility to compare your adjustments.

Step 2: Open the Color Balance Tool

With the duplicate layer selected, navigate to the top menu and select Colors > Color Balance.... The Color Balance dialog will appear, displaying three range options (Shadows, Midtones, and Highlights) along with three color sliders: Cyan/Red, Magenta/Green, and Yellow/Blue. Ensure that the Preserve Luminosity box is checked at the bottom to maintain the image's original exposure while altering colors.

Step 3: Neutralize the Midtones

Most ambient fluorescent light affects the midtones of an interior shot.

  1. Under the Select Range to Adjust section, select Midtones.
  2. Locate the Magenta / Green slider.
  3. Drag the slider slightly toward the Magenta side (to the left, into negative values).
  4. Move the slider in small increments, typically between -5 and -20, until the overall green wash disappears from neutral surfaces like white walls, floors, or furniture.

Step 4: Balance the Shadows and Highlights

Fluorescent light also seeps into dark corners and bright areas, requiring separate fine-tuning:

  • Shadows: Select the Shadows radio button. Gently push the Magenta / Green slider slightly toward Magenta (often between -2 and -10). If the shadows appear muddy, you may also nudge the Cyan / Red slider slightly toward Red to warm up dark recesses.
  • Highlights: Select the Highlights radio button. Push the Magenta / Green slider toward Magenta with a light touch. Be cautious here; over-correcting highlights will turn white light fixtures and glossy reflections noticeably pink.

Step 5: Fine-Tune Overall Temperature

Fluorescent bulbs can be either "cool white" (leaning blue-green) or "warm white" (leaning yellow-green). After canceling out the green:

  • If the room looks too cold or blue, select Midtones and move the Yellow / Blue slider slightly toward Yellow.
  • If the room looks too orange or muddy, nudge the Yellow / Blue slider slightly toward Blue.

Step 6: Preview and Confirm

Toggle the Preview checkbox on and off inside the dialog window to evaluate your changes against the uncorrected photo. Once the whites look neutral and the interior lighting appears natural, click OK to apply the color balance settings.
