Calculate Selection Pixel Area in GIMP

Measuring the exact pixel area of an active selection in GIMP is easily achieved using the built-in Histogram analytical dialog. When a selection is active, this dialog dynamically updates its statistical readouts to isolate only the pixels contained within that boundary, allowing you to quickly read the precise pixel count of any complex shape or region without relying on external plugins or manual math.

Step 1: Open the Histogram Dialog

To access the analytical readout, open the Histogram dockable dialog:

  1. Navigate to the top menu and click on Windows.
  2. Hover over Dockable Dialogs.
  3. Select Histogram from the list.

The Histogram panel will open, displaying the tonal distribution and statistical data of the active layer.

Step 2: Make Your Selection

Use any of GIMP's selection tools—such as the Rectangle Select, Ellipse Select, Free Select (Lasso), or Fuzzy Select (Magic Wand)—to outline the target area on your canvas. As soon as the "marching ants" appear, GIMP automatically confines the data displayed in the Histogram dialog to that specific region.

Step 3: Read the Exact Pixel Area

Look at the numerical statistics listed directly below the visual graph in the Histogram dialog:

  1. Locate the entry labeled Pixels.
  2. The number displayed next to Pixels indicates the exact total count of pixels encompassed by your active selection.

Because one pixel equals one square pixel in area measurement, this figure represents the exact surface area of your selected region.

Considerations for Feathered Selections

If your selection has soft or feathered edges, the Histogram dialog calculates the pixel count based on effective coverage. Pixels with partial transparency or partial selection weight are factored into the statistical count proportionally. For an absolute integer count of fully enclosed pixels, ensure that the Feather edges option in your selection tool's settings is disabled before drawing your selection boundaries.
