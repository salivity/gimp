How to Create a Contrast Mask in GIMP

A custom darkroom contrast mask in GIMP allows photographers to manage dynamic range, tame high-contrast scenes, and preserve delicate highlight and shadow details without producing harsh digital artifacts. This guide outlines the precise, step-by-step workflow for replicating traditional darkroom masking techniques by generating an inverted, blurred monochrome layer and blending it back into your original image.

Step 1: Duplicate the Base Image

Open your target image in GIMP. Navigate to the Layers panel, right-click on your base image layer (typically named Background), and select Duplicate Layer (or press Shift + Ctrl + D). Rename this new layer to Contrast Mask.

Step 2: Desaturate the Layer

A contrast mask relies solely on luminance data to adjust tone values.

  1. Select the Contrast Mask layer.
  2. Go to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate...
  3. Choose the Luminance mode from the dropdown menu and click OK.

Step 3: Invert the Tonal Values

To counteract excessive contrast, the mask must hold the inverse tonal values of the original exposure.

  1. Ensure the Contrast Mask layer is still active.
  2. Go to Colors > Invert (or press Ctrl + I). Highlights will become dark, and shadows will become bright.

Step 4: Apply Gaussian Blur

Blurring the mask prevents sharp edges and halo artifacts, ensuring that tonal adjustments apply across broad areas rather than micro-details.

  1. Navigate to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur...
  2. Adjust the Size X and Size Y radius. A standard starting point is between 30 to 80 pixels, depending on the resolution of your image. Higher-resolution files require a larger radius.
  3. Click OK once the fine details are obscured, leaving only broad tonal transitions.

Step 5: Set the Layer Blending Mode

Blend the mask into the underlying photo to compress or redistribute the dynamic range.

  1. In the Layers panel, change the Mode dropdown of the Contrast Mask layer from Normal to Overlay or Soft Light.
    • Overlay produces a more aggressive correction.
    • Soft Light yields a gentler, more natural transition.

Step 6: Fine-Tune the Mask

After applying the blend mode, adjust the strength of the mask to match the scene's requirements:

  • Opacity: Lower the layer opacity to reduce the intensity of the effect (often settled between 40% and 70%).
  • Curves Adjustment: Go to Colors > Curves... with the mask layer selected to adjust the mask's contrast directly, targeted at specific shadows or highlights.
  • Layer Mask: Add a black or white layer mask to paint the effect in or out of specific localized areas of your photograph.
