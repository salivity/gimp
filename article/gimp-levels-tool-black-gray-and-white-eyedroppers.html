GIMP Levels Tool: Black, Gray, and White Eyedroppers

The Levels dialog in GIMP features three eyedropper tools designed to correct tone, contrast, and color balance by sampling pixels directly from your canvas. By setting the black point, gray point, and white point, these pickers automatically remap pixel values across the color channels, stretching the dynamic range and eliminating unwanted color casts in just a few clicks.

The Black Point Eyedropper

The black point eyedropper (represented by a dropper over a black square) sets the darkest threshold of the image. When you click on a pixel with this tool:

  • The sampled color value is remapped to pure black (\(RGB: 0, 0, 0\)).
  • Any pixel in the image darker than the sampled tone is clipped to absolute black.
  • The tones between the sampled point and the midtones are stretched downward, deepening the shadows and boosting overall contrast.
  • If the sampled pixel had a slight color tint, the individual Red, Green, and Blue input levels are adjusted to neutralize shadow color contamination.

The Gray Point Eyedropper

The gray point eyedropper (represented by a dropper over a gray square) serves as a color balance and neutral tone corrector. When you click on a pixel that should theoretically be neutral gray:

  • GIMP evaluates the Red, Green, and Blue values of the selected pixel.
  • It adjusts the gamma (the middle input slider) of each color channel individually until the Red, Green, and Blue values are balanced equally.
  • This process neutralizes color casts throughout the midtones without altering the absolute black or white anchor points of the image.

The White Point Eyedropper

The white point eyedropper (represented by a dropper over a white square) sets the brightest threshold of the image. When you click on a highlight area with this tool:

  • The sampled color value is remapped to pure white (\(RGB: 255, 255, 255\)).
  • Any pixel in the image brighter than the sampled point is clipped to pure white.
  • Tonal values between the midtones and the sampled point are stretched upward, brightening the highlights.
  • Sampling a tinted highlight shifts the individual channel limits, correcting highlight color casts.

Together, these three tools provide an efficient way to expand flat tonal ranges, restore true shadows and highlights, and correct the color temperature of an image without manually tweaking individual histogram sliders.
