GIMP Incremental Painting Mode Explained

This article explains the function and practical application of the Incremental painting mode checkbox in GIMP. You will learn how this setting alters brush behavior, how it differs from default painting operations, and when to enable or disable it in your digital editing workflow.

What Does Incremental Mode Do?

The Incremental checkbox in GIMP controls how opacity accumulates within a single, continuous brush stroke.

When painting with an opacity level below 100%, the Incremental setting determines whether overlapping areas within the same stroke will stack color on top of each other or maintain a consistent, flat opacity until the mouse button or stylus is released.

Non-Incremental Mode (Default Behavior)

By default, the Incremental box is unchecked. In this standard mode:

  • Uniform Opacity: If your brush opacity is set to 30%, you can scrub back and forth over the same area without releasing the mouse button, and the opacity will never exceed 30%.
  • Single-Layer Calculation: GIMP treats the entire continuous stroke as a single application of paint with a strict maximum opacity cap.
  • Building Up Color: To increase opacity or darken the area, you must release the mouse button (or lift your stylus) and begin a completely new stroke over the existing one.

This mode is ideal for laying down flat, even tones or transparent glazes without worrying about creating accidental dark patches where the brush overlaps itself.

Incremental Mode Enabled

When you check the Incremental box:

  • Cumulative Opacity: The brush deposits color incrementally with every movement (or dab). If your brush crosses over a section of the canvas it just touched during the same stroke, the color stacks and becomes progressively more opaque.
  • Mimicking Physical Media: This behavior functions similarly to physical markers, watercolors, or traditional pencils, where continuously rubbing back and forth in one spot deposits more pigment.
  • Reaching Maximum Value: Even with a low opacity setting, overlapping the stroke repeatedly within a single motion will eventually build up to 100% full opacity.

Where to Find the Setting

The Incremental checkbox is located in the Tool Options dockable dialog. It is available when using paint-based tools, including:

  • Paintbrush
  • Pencil
  • Eraser
  • Airbrush (where rate and pressure also influence buildup)

When to Use Incremental Mode

  • Shading and Blending: Enable Incremental when you want to quickly build up shadow, depth, or highlights without repeatedly clicking or lifting your pen from the tablet.
  • Sketching: Enable it to simulate the natural feeling of sketching with graphite or ink markers.
  • Even Coloring: Disable it when you need consistent, smooth coverage over an entire shape or selection at a specific transparency level.
