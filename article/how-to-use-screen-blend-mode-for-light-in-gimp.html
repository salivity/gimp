How to Use Screen Blend Mode for Light in GIMP

The Screen blend mode in GIMP is one of the most effective tools for compositing lighting effects—such as lens flares, bokeh, fog, sparks, and sunbeams—onto an image. This blend mode works mathematically by inverting the colors, multiplying them, and inverting them again, which causes pure black areas to become completely transparent while preserving and brightening the lighter pixels. This guide explains how to properly apply the Screen mode to overlay light textures seamlessly without the need for complex manual selections or masking.

Step 1: Open Your Images as Layers

  1. Launch GIMP and open your base photograph by selecting File > Open.
  2. Add your light texture on top of the base image by going to File > Open as Layers.
  3. In the Layers panel on the right side of the screen, ensure the light texture layer sits directly above the base image layer.

Step 2: Set the Blend Mode to Screen

  1. Click on the light texture layer in the Layers panel to select it.
  2. Locate the Mode dropdown menu at the top of the Layers panel (default is usually set to Normal).
  3. Open the dropdown menu and select Screen.
  4. The black background of the texture will instantly disappear, leaving only the bright elements superimposed over your base image.

Step 3: Remove Residual Gray Backgrounds with Levels

Light textures that do not have a pure black background (#000000) may leave a faint gray haze over your base image. To eliminate this:

  1. With the texture layer selected, go to Colors > Levels.
  2. Under the Input Levels histogram, click and drag the black (leftmost) slider slightly to the right.
  3. Dragging this slider pushes near-black tones to pure black, completely removing any unwanted gray cast and making the texture blend naturally.
  4. Click OK to apply the adjustment.

Step 4: Position and Scale the Texture

  1. Select the Scale Tool (Shift + S) from the toolbox to resize the light texture to match the composition of your scene.
  2. Select the Move Tool (M) to position the light source naturally, such as placing a sun flare along the edges or over an existing light source in the background.

Step 5: Adjust Intensity and Soften Edges

  1. Lower Opacity: If the light effect appears overly harsh, decrease the Opacity slider at the top of the Layers panel to soften its appearance.
  2. Add a Layer Mask: To remove hard texture edges or localized light spill, right-click the texture layer and select Add Layer Mask > White (full opacity). Use a soft, black paintbrush with low opacity to gently paint away areas where the light texture should not appear.

Step 6: Match Color Tones (Optional)

To integrate the lighting realistically, match the texture's color temperature to your base image:

  1. Go to Colors > Color Temperature or Colors > Hue-Saturation.
  2. Adjust the sliders to warm up, cool down, or shift the hue of the light effect until it harmonizes with the surrounding environment.
