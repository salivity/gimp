How to Make Realistic Water Drops in GIMP

Simulating realistic water drops in GIMP involves recreating how transparent liquids interact with light through refraction, shadows, and specular highlights. This tutorial explains how to construct believable water droplets on any surface using GIMP's selection tools, layer blend modes, gradients, and brush tools to achieve accurate light reflections and depth.

Step 1: Create the Droplet Selection

  1. Open your background surface image in GIMP.
  2. Create a new transparent layer named Water Droplet.
  3. Select the Ellipse Select Tool (or the Paths Tool for irregular, natural shapes) and draw the outline of your droplet on the new layer.

Step 2: Add the Cast Shadow

  1. Keep the selection active.
  2. Go to Filters > Light and Shadow > Drop Shadow.
  3. Set the shadow angle to match the natural light direction of your background image.
  4. Set the blur radius low (between 5 and 15 pixels depending on canvas resolution) and lower the opacity to around 50–60%. This creates the small, sharp shadow directly beneath the droplet.

Step 3: Simulate Refraction and Base Shading

Water refracts light, causing the side closest to the light source to be darker inside, while the opposite base glows.

  1. Return to the Water Droplet layer with the selection still active.
  2. Select the Gradient Tool (G), set the gradient to Foreground to Transparent (black foreground), and choose the Linear shape.
  3. Drag the gradient from the light-facing side toward the center to create a subtle inner shadow along the top edge.
  4. Set the Water Droplet layer blend mode to Overlay or Soft Light so the underlying texture shows through realistically.

Step 4: Create the Bottom Internal Glow

  1. Create a new transparent layer above the droplet layer and name it Bottom Glow.
  2. With the droplet selection still active, select a soft round Paintbrush (P) with white color.
  3. Paint a soft, curved stroke along the inside edge opposite the light source (usually the bottom edge).
  4. Lower the layer opacity to roughly 40–70% to mimic the focused light exiting the droplet.

Step 5: Paint the Specular Highlights

  1. Create a new transparent layer named Highlights.
  2. Switch to a small, hard-edged Paintbrush with pure white color.
  3. Click once or paint a small crescent shape near the top edge directly facing the light source. This represents the primary reflection of the light source.
  4. Slightly reduce the brush size and add a much smaller, faint white dot on the opposite side to simulate secondary reflection/bounce light.

Step 6: Refine and Duplicate

  1. Deselect the active area (Select > None or Shift + Ctrl + A).
  2. Group all droplet-related layers into a single Layer Group.
  3. Duplicate the group and use the Unified Transform Tool (Shift + T) to scale, rotate, and scatter multiple water droplets across the surface for a natural appearance.
