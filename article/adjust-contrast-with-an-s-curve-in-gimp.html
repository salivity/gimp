Adjust Contrast with an S-Curve in GIMP

Adjusting the contrast of an image using an S-curve in GIMP is one of the most effective ways to add depth and punch to your photos without losing control over tonal ranges. By modifying the diagonal line of the Value channel into a subtle "S" shape, you selectively darken the shadows while brightening the highlights. This guide walks you directly through opening the Curves tool, manipulating the curve points, and fine-tuning your adjustments for natural results.

Step 1: Open the Curves Tool

  1. Open your image in GIMP.
  2. In the top menu, go to Colors and select Curves....
  3. A dialog box will appear displaying a histogram overlaid with a diagonal linear graph running from the bottom-left to the top-right.

Step 2: Set the Channel to Value

  1. Locate the Channel dropdown menu at the top of the Curves dialog.
  2. Ensure it is set to Value.
    • The Value channel controls the overall brightness and luminosity of the image across all color channels simultaneously, rather than adjusting individual red, green, or blue colors.

Step 3: Understand the Graph

  • Bottom-Left: Represents the shadows and absolute black (input 0).
  • Center: Represents the midtones (input 128).
  • Top-Right: Represents the highlights and pure white (input 255).
  • The horizontal axis represents the original input values, while the vertical axis represents the new output values.

Step 4: Form the S-Curve

  1. Lower the Shadows: Click on the diagonal line roughly one-quarter of the way from the bottom-left corner. Drag this point slightly downward. This lowers the output value for the dark tones, making the shadows deeper.
  2. Raise the Highlights: Click on the diagonal line roughly three-quarters of the way up toward the top-right corner. Drag this point slightly upward. This raises the output value for the lighter tones, making highlights brighter.
  3. Anchor the Midtones (Optional): If you want to keep your midtone exposure unchanged, click the exact center of the line to place an anchor point, ensuring the curve passes directly through the original midtone coordinate.

The resulting line will resemble a gentle letter "S."

Step 5: Evaluate and Fine-Tune

  • Check for Clipping: Avoid pulling the points too far toward the extreme top or bottom edges, as this can clip details, turning shadows into muddy black patches or blowing out highlights.
  • Use the Preview Option: Toggle the Preview checkbox on and off to compare the adjusted image against the original. Alternatively, check Split view to see a side-by-side comparison directly on the canvas.
  • Adjust Individual Points: You can select any control point on the curve and adjust its position using the arrow keys on your keyboard for precise increments.

Step 6: Apply the Adjustment

Once you are satisfied with the tonal balance and contrast, click OK at the bottom of the Curves window to apply the change permanently to the active layer.
