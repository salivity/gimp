Frequency Separation in GIMP for Skin Retouching

Frequency separation is an essential skin retouching technique that splits an image into two distinct layers: a low-frequency layer containing color, tone, and transitions, and a high-frequency layer containing fine details like pores, blemishes, and texture. By separating these components in GIMP, you can smooth skin tones without blurring away vital texture, or remove blemishes without leaving blotchy discolorations. This guide outlines the exact, non-destructive procedure for creating a frequency separation layer stack in GIMP.

1. Prepare the Base Layers

  1. Open your portrait in GIMP.
  2. In the Layers panel, duplicate the base image layer twice.
  3. Rename the middle layer to Low Frequency.
  4. Rename the top layer to High Frequency.

2. Isolate the Low-Frequency (Color and Tone) Layer

  1. Hide the High Frequency layer by clicking the eye icon next to it.
  2. Select the Low Frequency layer.
  3. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur...
  4. Adjust the Size X and Size Y radius. Increase the blur just until the fine skin textures and pores disappear, leaving only the broader colors and lighting transitions visible (typically between 3 to 10 pixels, depending on image resolution).
  5. Click OK to apply the blur.

3. Extract the High-Frequency (Texture) Layer

  1. Turn the visibility of the High Frequency layer back on and select it.
  2. Change the layer mode of the High Frequency layer from Normal to Grain extract. The canvas will turn neutral gray, displaying only the fine surface textures.
  3. Right-click anywhere in the Layers panel and select New from Visible.
  4. Rename this new layer to Texture.
  5. Delete the temporary High Frequency layer, as it is no longer needed.
  6. Select the Texture layer, move it to the very top of the stack, and change its layer mode to Grain merge.

4. Verify and Group the Layer Stack

  1. Create a new layer group by clicking the folder icon at the bottom of the Layers panel and name it Frequency Separation.
  2. Drag both the Texture layer and the Low Frequency layer inside this group, keeping Texture on top.
  3. Toggle the visibility of the entire group off and on. The image inside the group should look completely identical to the original unedited photo.

5. Retouching the Separated Layers

  • Fixing Texture: Select the Texture layer and use the Clone Tool or Healing Tool with a hard-edged brush to clean up blemishes, flyaway hairs, and wrinkles without altering the underlying skin tones.
  • Fixing Tone and Transitions: Select the Low Frequency layer and use a soft-edged brush, the Smudge Tool, or the Lasso Tool paired with a gentle Gaussian Blur to smooth out uneven shadows, redness, or blotchy makeup without degrading the skin's natural pores.
