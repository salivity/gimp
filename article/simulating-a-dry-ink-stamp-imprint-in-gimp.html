Simulating a Dry-Ink Stamp Imprint in GIMP

Simulating an authentic dry-ink stamp effect in GIMP involves designing a crisp base graphic, degrading its structure with procedural noise, and using layer masks to replicate uneven pressure and paper texture. This guide outlines the exact, step-by-step workflow required to transform clean text and shapes into a realistic, weathered stamp imprint using GIMP's built-in tools.

Step 1: Design the Base Stamp Artwork

  1. Create a new transparent layer (Layer > New Layer) above your background or document.
  2. Select a bold, sans-serif or slab-serif font and type your desired text.
  3. Draw a circular or rectangular border around the text using the Ellipse Select or Rectangle Select tool, followed by Edit > Stroke Selection (set line width between 4px and 8px depending on canvas resolution).
  4. Right-click the stamp text layer in the Layers panel and choose Merge Down so the text and border exist on a single transparent layer.

Step 2: Roughen the Edges

Real rubber stamps produce irregular, slightly blurred, or bleeding contours rather than razor-sharp vector edges.

  1. Select the stamp layer and apply a slight blur by going to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur (set radius between 1.0 and 2.5 px).
  2. Go to Filters > Distorts > Ripple to add micro-variations. Set the Period to a low value (approx. 5–10) and the Amplitude to 1–3 px to make the edges subtly uneven without warping the shapes drastically.

Step 3: Create Uneven Ink Coverage with a Layer Mask

Dry ink stamps leave voids where the rubber fails to make full contact with the paper fibers.

  1. Right-click the stamp layer and select Add Layer Mask, choosing White (full opacity).
  2. Ensure the layer mask is selected (indicated by a white outline in the Layers panel).
  3. Go to Filters > Render > Noise > Solid Noise. Adjust the X/Y Size sliders until you see a cloudy pattern of dark and light patches across the stamp, then click OK.
  4. To heighten the contrast of the dry spots, go to Colors > Levels while the mask is selected. Drag the black and white input sliders toward the center to create sharp, realistic voids in the ink coverage.

Step 4: Apply Grain and Micro-Distress

  1. With the layer mask still active, go to Filters > Noise > CIE lch Noise (or RGB Noise).
  2. Increase the noise level slightly to create granular speckles throughout the imprint.
  3. Use a textured dry-media brush (such as the default Chalk or Bristles preset) with black paint at 30–50% opacity to manually dab away areas where you want heavier fading, typically along the outer edges or corners.

Step 5: Adjust Color and Blend Mode

  1. Ensure the stamp layer uses an authentic ink color (such as faded navy, muted crimson, or deep charcoal) using Colors > Colorize or Colors > Hue-Chroma.
  2. Change the stamp layer's blend mode from Normal to Multiply in the Layers panel so underlying paper textures and fibers show through the ink.
  3. Lower the layer opacity to between 80% and 90% to emulate the natural transparency of dried stamping ink.
