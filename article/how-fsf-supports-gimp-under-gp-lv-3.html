How FSF Supports GIMP Under GPLv3

The Free Software Foundation (FSF) provides the critical legal, organizational, and philosophical framework that enables the GNU Image Manipulation Program (GIMP) to be distributed and maintained under the GNU General Public License version 3 (GPLv3). By authoring and maintaining the GPLv3, providing license compliance assistance, defending software freedom, and offering distribution infrastructure, the FSF ensures that GIMP remains universally accessible, modifiable, and protected against proprietary exploitation.

Stewardship and Maintenance of the GPLv3 License

The FSF is the creator and legal steward of the GNU General Public License. When GIMP transitioned parts of its codebase to GPLv3 (and LGPLv3 for its core libraries, BABL and GEGL), it relied directly on the legal architecture drafted by the FSF. The GPLv3 specifically protects GIMP from modern software threats by including robust clauses against digital restrictions management (DRM or "tivoization") and explicit protections against predatory software patent claims. The FSF maintains the definitive interpretation of this license, eliminating legal ambiguity for GIMP developers and downstream distributors.

Legal Compliance and License Enforcement

Through its Licensing and Compliance Lab, the FSF helps safeguard projects like GIMP from copyright infringement and license violations. When third parties repackage or redistribute GIMP without releasing the corresponding source code—a direct violation of the GPLv3—the FSF offers guidance and established procedures to restore compliance. While individual GIMP contributors hold the copyright to their respective code contributions, the FSF’s established legal precedents and compliance standards provide the muscle needed to deter companies from turning GIMP into closed-source, proprietary derivatives.

GNU Project Association and Governance

GIMP was originally conceived as the "General Image Manipulation Program" but was renamed the "GNU Image Manipulation Program" upon becoming an official part of the GNU Project, which was founded by the FSF. As an official GNU package, GIMP adheres to the technical and ethical software guidelines established by the FSF. This affiliation gives GIMP institutional credibility, aligning its release cycles and licensing changes with the overarching mission of the global free software movement.

Distribution Support and Directory Listing

The FSF aids in the visibility and distribution of GIMP through the Free Software Directory, a comprehensive database of verified free software packages maintained by the foundation. The FSF audits programs submitted to the directory to verify that they strictly comply with free licenses like the GPLv3 and contain no non-free dependencies. By certifying GIMP as fully compliant software, the FSF ensures users and enterprise environments can download and deploy the application with full confidence in its legal standing and freedom.

Public Education and Legal Documentation

Distributing complex software under the GPLv3 involves navigating intricate dependency chains, plugins, and third-party libraries. The FSF supports the GIMP community by producing extensive legal documentation, licensing FAQs, and compatibility matrices. This clarifies how developers can create and distribute GIMP plugins, integrate external scripts, and package the software across various operating systems without inadvertently compromising the protections guaranteed by the GPLv3.
