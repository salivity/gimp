Create Realistic Light Beams in GIMP with Motion Blur

This guide provides a step-by-step walkthrough on how to generate realistic light beams—often called god rays or volumetric lighting—using GIMP's Motion Blur filter. By painting basic light shapes, applying directional or zoom motion blur, and adjusting blend modes and masks, you can add atmospheric depth and cinematic lighting to any photograph or digital artwork.

Step 1: Prepare the Image and Light Layer

  1. Open your image in GIMP (File > Open).
  2. Create a new transparent layer above your base image by clicking the New Layer icon in the Layers panel or selecting Layer > New Layer. Name it "Light Beams".
  3. Set the layer mode of the "Light Beams" layer to Screen, Addition, or Dodge to allow the light to naturally blend with the background.

Step 2: Draw the Light Source

  1. Select the Paintbrush Tool (P) with a soft-edged round brush, or use the Free Select Tool (F) to outline the area where the light originates (such as a window, a gap in the trees, or the sun).
  2. Choose a pure white or warm off-white color (#FFF8E7).
  3. Paint spots, dots, or diagonal stripes across the origin area. These shapes do not need to be precise, as the blur filter will stretch them into smooth rays.

Step 3: Apply the Motion Blur Filter

  1. Go to the top menu and select Filters > Blur > Motion Blur (Linear) or Filters > Blur > Motion Blur (Zoom).
    • Linear Motion Blur: Best for parallel, directional light beams streaming from a side angle. Set the Angle to match the direction of the light source and increase the Length significantly (e.g., 200–500 pixels) to stretch the brush strokes into beams.
    • Zoom Motion Blur: Best for radial rays expanding from a single point (like the sun). Set the center point coordinates to the position of your light source and increase the Factor or Length to radiate the beams outward.
  2. Click OK to apply the filter. If the beams are not long enough, repeat the filter by pressing Ctrl + F.

Step 4: Soften and Refine the Beams

  1. If the edges of the light beams appear too sharp or pixelated, apply a slight Gaussian blur by selecting Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur. Set a small radius (5–15 px) to soften the rays.
  2. Lower the Opacity of the "Light Beams" layer in the Layers panel (typically between 30% and 70%) so the effect blends subtly into the environment.

Step 5: Add a Layer Mask for Realism

  1. Right-click the "Light Beams" layer and select Add Layer Mask, choosing White (full opacity).
  2. Select the Gradient Tool (G) with a black-to-transparent or black-to-white gradient.
  3. Drag the gradient from where the light ends toward where it begins to make the beams naturally fade out as they travel further from the source.
  4. Use a soft black brush on the layer mask to erase light rays that should be blocked by foreground objects.

Step 6: Adjust Light Color (Optional)

  1. If the light requires a specific color tone (such as a golden sunset or cool moonlight), go to Colors > Color Temperature or Colors > Colorize.
  2. Adjust the hue and saturation sliders until the beam matches the ambient lighting of the original scene.
