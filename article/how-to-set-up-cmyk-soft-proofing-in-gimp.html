How to Set Up CMYK Soft Proofing in GIMP

Setting up CMYK soft-proofing in GIMP allows you to simulate how your RGB digital artwork will look when printed using specific ink standards and paper types. By configuring an ICC printer color profile in GIMP's color management settings, you can preview color shifts and identify out-of-gamut colors directly on your screen without altering the original image data.

Step 1: Obtain a CMYK ICC Profile

GIMP does not include proprietary CMYK profiles by default. You must obtain an ICC profile matching your target print standard:

  • Download standard press profiles (such as SWOP, FOGRA39, or GRACoL) from your commercial printer or color organization websites like Adobe, ECI (European Color Initiative), or IDEAlliance.
  • Save the .icc or .icm file to a known directory on your computer or your operating system's default color profiles folder.

Step 2: Configure Color Management Preferences

  1. Open GIMP.
  2. Go to Edit > Preferences (on macOS, GIMP > Preferences).
  3. In the left sidebar of the Preferences window, select Color Management.
  4. Set the Image display mode to Color managed display.
  5. Locate the Soft-proofing color profile dropdown menu, click it, select Select color profile from disk..., and browse to your downloaded CMYK .icc file.
  6. Under Rendering intent for soft-proofing, choose Perceptual or Relative colorimetric (Relative colorimetric is standard for print proofs).
  7. (Optional) Check Mark out of gamut colors and choose an Out of gamut color (such as bright magenta) to easily highlight colors that standard CMYK printing cannot reproduce.
  8. Click OK to save the preferences.

Step 3: Enable Soft-Proofing in the Image Window

Once configured globally or for the project, soft-proofing can be toggled on and off per image view:

  1. Open your image project in GIMP.
  2. Click View in the top menu bar.
  3. Select Color Management > Soft-Proofing (or press the keyboard shortcut configured for soft-proofing).
  4. The display will immediately update to simulate the CMYK color space.
  5. To check which colors exceed the CMYK gamut, go to View > Color Management and check Out of Gamut Warning.
