GIMP Linear vs Perceptual Gamma Layer Modes

In GIMP, layer blend modes can be calculated using either Linear or Perceptual gamma, a setting that directly dictates the mathematical space in which color and light values interact. While Perceptual gamma calculates blends according to the non-linear sRGB tone curve designed around human vision, Linear gamma calculates blends in a physically accurate, linear space that simulates real-world optical light physics. Choosing between these modes determines whether your composites feature soft, natural light falloffs or the punchy, high-contrast results common in traditional digital editing.

The Core Difference: How Light is Calculated

Standard digital images are usually stored in a non-linear color space (sRGB) with a gamma curve of approximately 2.2. This perceptual encoding allocates more data to darker tones because human eyes are more sensitive to subtle differences in shadows than in bright highlights.

  • Perceptual Mode (Non-Linear): Performs mathematical calculations directly on the gamma-encoded values. Because the numbers do not correspond to actual photon counts, combining two pixels mathematically distorts the light balance.
  • Linear Mode (Radiometric): Temporarily removes the gamma curve, performs the blend operation on linear values that represent physical light intensities, and then converts the result back for display.

Visual Impact on Blending

The mathematical difference leads to distinct visual outcomes depending on the operation:

  • Edge Blending and Anti-Aliasing: In Perceptual mode, overlapping soft edges or partially transparent colors often produce an unnatural dark border or "dirty" fringe. Linear mode blends edge transitions smoothly without introducing dark halos, maintaining clean transparency.
  • Gradients and Light Sources: Blending luminous colors (such as red and green) in Linear mode correctly produces a bright yellow in the overlapping area, mimicking how light behaves in reality. In Perceptual mode, the overlapping area often looks desaturated, muddy, or darker than the source colors.
  • Lighting Modes (Multiply, Screen, Overlay): Operations like Multiply and Screen produce significantly more realistic illumination and shadow effects in Linear mode. For example, adding light via the Screen or Addition mode in Linear space avoids blowing out midtones prematurely.

Choosing the Right Mode

GIMP allows you to toggle this setting via the layer's blend mode dropdown by selecting either the regular (Linear) or legacy (Perceptual) variations, or by setting the layer group's blend space.

  • Use Linear Gamma when: Creating digital paintings, rendering optical effects (such as lens flares, glows, or depth-of-field blur), compositing photorealistic elements, or working with 3D renders. It produces cleaner blends, physically accurate color mixing, and halo-free edges.
  • Use Perceptual Gamma when: You need backward compatibility with older GIMP project files (prior to GIMP 2.10), want to match Adobe Photoshop’s standard blend behavior precisely, or specifically want the higher-contrast, punchier appearance characteristic of legacy digital image manipulation.
