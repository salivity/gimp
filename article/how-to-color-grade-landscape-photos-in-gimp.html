How to Color Grade Landscape Photos in GIMP

Color grading transforms a standard landscape photograph into a dramatic, cinematic image by manipulating tones, color harmony, and contrast. This guide provides a step-by-step workflow for achieving a cinematic look in GIMP using native tools such as Curves, Color Balance, Split Toning, and Vignette adjustments without degrading image quality.

1. Prepare and Correct the Base Image

Open your landscape photograph in GIMP and duplicate the base layer (Layer > Duplicate Layer) to ensure non-destructive editing.

  1. Go to Colors > Exposure to fix any major underexposure or overexposure. Keep highlights intact and shadows detailed.
  2. Open Colors > Levels to set the black point and white point accurately, maximizing the dynamic range before applying stylistic color grades.

2. Establish Contrast with Tone Curves

Cinematic grading often uses slightly lifted blacks and controlled highlights to emulate film stocks.

  1. Navigate to Colors > Curves.
  2. Create a gentle "S-curve" on the Value channel to enhance midtone contrast.
  3. Slightly raise the bottom-left point (the black point) vertically to lift the deep blacks into a soft, matte charcoal tone.
  4. Slightly pull down the top-right point (the white point) to prevent blown-out highlights in the sky.

3. Apply Split Toning (Teal and Orange Balance)

A classic cinematic palette contrasts warm highlights with cool shadows.

  1. Go to Colors > Color Balance.
  2. Select Shadows and add Cyan and Blue to introduce cool, atmospheric tones to terrain, water, and shaded areas.
  3. Select Highlights and add Red and Yellow to infuse sunlight, clouds, and direct light with warm, golden hues.
  4. Adjust the Midtones subtly to bridge the transition naturally.

4. Refine Specific Foliage and Sky Hues

Landscape photos require fine control over greens, blues, and earth tones.

  1. Go to Colors > Hue-Saturation.
  2. Select the Green (G) channel to shift overly bright neon greens toward a moodier olive or emerald tone by adjusting Hue and lowering Saturation slightly.
  3. Select the Blue (B) or Cyan (C) channel to adjust the sky's intensity and hue to complement the overall color palette.

5. Add Depth with Directional Light and Vignette

Draw the viewer's eye into the center of the frame and enhance the dramatic lighting.

  1. Go to Filters > Light and Shadow > Vignette.
  2. Set the shape to Classic or Horizontal (to match the landscape horizon).
  3. Increase the Radius and soften the Fading so the darkening of the edges remains subtle and organic.

6. Final Sharpening and Export

  1. Create a merged layer of all adjustments by selecting Layer > New from Visible.
  2. Apply subtle detail enhancement via Filters > Enhance > High Pass, set the layer blending mode to Soft Light, and lower opacity to around 30–50%.
  3. Go to File > Export As and save the final image in your desired format (e.g., JPEG or TIFF with maximum quality).
