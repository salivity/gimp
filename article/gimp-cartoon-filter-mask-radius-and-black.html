GIMP Cartoon Filter: Mask Radius and Black

The Cartoon filter in GIMP transforms photographs into comic-style illustrations by simulating ink outlines and tonal shading. Achieving a clean, professional result depends entirely on how you balance two core parameters: Mask Radius and Percent Black. This guide breaks down the functions of both sliders, explains how they interact, and provides practical workflows to help you dial in the exact look you want without muddying your image.

Understanding the Two Parameters

The Cartoon filter (found under Filters > Artistic > Cartoon) works by analyzing the contrast between neighboring pixels to generate simulated ink outlines and dark shadows.

  • Mask Radius: This setting determines the width and spread of the filter's calculations. A lower radius limits the edge detection to tight, fine areas, resulting in thin, delicate lines that preserve small details. A higher radius broadens the sample area, producing thick, heavy brush strokes and generalized tonal patches while smoothing over finer textures.
  • Percent Black: This setting controls the density and quantity of black ink applied across the image. It acts as a threshold: lower percentages add ink only to the strongest contrast edges, while higher percentages force midtones and darker shades toward pure black, creating a dramatic, high-contrast graphic novel aesthetic.

The Dynamics of Balancing Both Settings

The two sliders directly influence one another. Adjusting one often requires counter-adjusting the other to prevent unwanted visual artifacts.

  1. High Mask Radius + High Percent Black: This combination produces a heavy, woodcut or stencil effect. Fine details disappear completely, replaced by large, aggressive slabs of pure black. Use this sparingly, typically for minimalist silhouettes or graphic logos.
  2. Low Mask Radius + High Percent Black: This yields sharp, intense line work similar to an intricate pen-and-ink drawing. The lines remain thin because of the low radius, but the high black percentage fills in almost every micro-contrast edge.
  3. High Mask Radius + Low Percent Black: This creates soft, posterized shading with sparse outlines. It works well for mimicking watercolor or soft cel-shading where harsh ink lines are not desired.
  4. Low Mask Radius + Low Percent Black: This results in a subtle, clean comic book style. Outlines are crisp and light, while the subject's base colors and gradients remain intact.

Recommended Workflow for Best Results

To achieve the proper balance on any image, adjust the sliders systematically rather than tweaking both at once:

  1. Set Percent Black to a Low Baseline: Start by moving the Percent Black slider down to approximately 0.100 to 0.200. This minimizes visual clutter while you define your lines.
  2. Dial In the Mask Radius: Increase the Mask Radius until the outlines match your desired thickness. For detailed portraits, keep the radius low (roughly 4.0 to 10.0) to avoid turning facial features into dark smudges. For landscapes or objects with bold shapes, higher values (15.0 to 30.0+) often yield better stylized silhouettes.
  3. Increase Percent Black to Set Weight: Once your outline structure is established, slowly raise the Percent Black slider. Stop when shadows have sufficient punch without swallowing critical highlights or textures.
  4. Counter Digital Noise: If the filter creates speckled, noisy black dots in smooth areas like skies or skin tones, lower the Percent Black slightly and increase the Mask Radius. Alternatively, apply a slight Gaussian Blur to the layer before running the Cartoon filter to smooth out pixel noise.
