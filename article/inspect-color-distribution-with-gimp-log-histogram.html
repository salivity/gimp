Inspect Color Distribution with GIMP Log Histogram

The Histogram dialog in GIMP provides a graphical representation of the tonal and color distribution within an image, allowing editors to evaluate exposure, contrast, and color balance accurately. While the default linear view highlights dominant pixel values, switching to logarithmic scale mode amplifies low-frequency data, making faint color casts, shadow noise, and subtle clipping readily visible. This guide explains how to access the Histogram dialog, activate the logarithmic scale, and analyze individual color channels to evaluate your image data effectively.

Open the Histogram Dialog

To open the Histogram dock, navigate to Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Histogram from the top menu bar. If you already have an image open, the dialog immediately populates with a graph representing the tonal range, stretching from pure black (level 0) on the far left to pure white (level 255) on the far right.

Switch to Logarithmic Scale Mode

GIMP defaults to a linear scale, where the vertical height of each point on the graph corresponds proportionally to the absolute number of pixels at that brightness level. Because prominent tones dominate this view, rare or subtle tonal values often appear flat against the baseline.

To switch views:

  1. Locate the two scale toggle buttons at the top right of the Histogram dialog.
  2. Click the Logarithmic Histogram button (represented by a curved logarithmic line icon next to the linear stair-step icon).

In logarithmic mode, the vertical axis measures values exponentially rather than linearly. This calculation drastically elevates rare pixel counts, exposing details across the tonal spectrum that were previously imperceptible.

Select and Analyze Color Channels

Use the Channel drop-down menu at the top of the Histogram dock to target specific color data:

  • RGB: Overlays the Red, Green, and Blue channels together, providing an immediate look at overall color harmony and balance.
  • Red, Green, or Blue: Isolates a single channel to evaluate its specific spread across the darks, midtones, and highlights.
  • Alpha: Displays transparency distribution if the active layer contains an alpha channel.

Reading the Logarithmic Distribution

With logarithmic scaling enabled, evaluate your color distribution using the following indicators:

  • Channel Clipping: Inspect the extreme edges at value 0 (shadows) and value 255 (highlights). A sharp spike clinging to either wall indicates clipped color data. In logarithmic mode, even a tiny cluster of clipped pixels will produce a distinct vertical line, alerting you to blown highlights or crushed shadows in that specific channel.
  • Color Cast Identification: Compare the peaks of the individual Red, Green, and Blue channels. If one color curve extends significantly further into the dark values (left side) than the others, a color cast exists in the shadows. Conversely, skewing toward the right indicates a tint in the highlights.
  • Tonal Gaps and Posterization: Check for vertical blank spaces or "comb-like" teeth in the distribution. These gaps reveal missing tonal values and data loss, which can cause color banding during heavy manipulation.

Applying Adjustments Based on Findings

Once you have identified color imbalances using the logarithmic histogram, open Colors > Levels or Colors > Curves to make corrections. Both tools include the same logarithmic display toggle, allowing you to monitor the redistribution of colors in real time as you adjust input levels, output ranges, and individual color curves.
