How to Wrap Logos on Fabric Folds in GIMP

This guide explains how to realistically wrap brand logos over the contours, wrinkles, and folds of fabric mockup templates in GIMP. By extracting the fabric's luminosity data to create a displacement map, you can physically warp the logo's geometry to follow the surface structure. Combining this distortion with luminance-based layer blending and masking ensures your graphic retains the natural highlights, shadows, and woven textures of the underlying garment.

Step 1: Prepare the Luminosity Map Layer

  1. Open your fabric mockup image in GIMP.
  2. In the Layers panel, right-click the base fabric layer and select Duplicate Layer. Name this layer Luminosity Map.
  3. With Luminosity Map selected, navigate to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate... and select Luminance to convert the layer into a pure grayscale representation of the fabric's light and dark areas.
  4. Go to Colors > Levels... to increase the contrast. Drag the black input slider toward the center to deepen the fold shadows, and drag the white input slider inward to brighten the ridges. Click OK.
  5. Hide this layer by clicking the eye icon next to it in the Layers panel. This layer will serve as the mathematical source for warping your graphic.

Step 2: Place and Align the Brand Logo

  1. Go to File > Open as Layers... and select your transparent PNG or vector logo.
  2. Use the Scale Tool (Shift + S) and Rotate Tool (Shift + R) to adjust the size and angle to match the garment's perspective.
  3. Right-click the logo layer in the Layers panel and choose Layer to Image Size. This matches the layer dimensions to your canvas, which is essential for accurate displacement mapping.

Step 3: Warp the Logo Using the Displace Filter

  1. Ensure the logo layer is selected.
  2. Navigate to Filters > Map > Displace....
  3. In the Displace dialog, set both the X Displacement and Y Displacement sources to your Luminosity Map layer.
  4. Adjust the horizontal and vertical displacement values (typically between 5 and 20, depending on image resolution). Watch the preview: the logo will shift along the light and dark gradients of the wrinkles.
  5. Click OK to apply the physical distortion.

Step 4: Blend Fabric Shadows and Highlights

To make the logo look printed or embroidered into the weave rather than floating on top, map the fabric's natural lighting back over the warped graphic:

  1. Duplicate your original base fabric layer and move it to the very top of the layer stack.
  2. Convert this duplicate to grayscale via Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate....
  3. Change the layer mode from Normal to Multiply or Soft Light to cast the fold shadows directly onto the logo.
  4. Right-click the logo layer, select Alpha to Selection, click the top desaturated fabric layer, and click Layer > Mask > Add Layer Mask.... Choose Selection and click Add. This confines the shading strictly within the logo boundaries.

Step 5: Match Texture and Depth

  1. Select your distorted logo layer and lower the opacity slightly (between 90% and 95%) so subtle surface texture shows through.
  2. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur... and apply a micro-blur of 0.5 to 1.0 pixels to remove digital vector sharpness, simulating how real ink blends into porous textiles.
