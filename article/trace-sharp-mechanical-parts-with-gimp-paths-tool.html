Trace Sharp Mechanical Parts with GIMP Paths Tool

Tracing sharp, geometric mechanical parts in GIMP requires absolute precision to preserve rigid edges, accurate angles, and distinct corner points. This guide provides a straightforward method for using the Paths tool to create exact vector outlines around mechanical components. By utilizing deliberate node placement, turning off unintended curve handles, and leveraging alignment aids, you can produce clean, hard-surface selections and strokes for technical drawings or clean cutouts.

1. Set Up Alignment Aids

Mechanical parts rely on exact angles and parallel lines. Prepare your canvas to assist the Paths tool:

  • Zoom in close: Zoom into your image at 200% to 800% (+ key) to locate the exact apex of every angle.
  • Enable Guides: Drag guides from the horizontal and vertical rulers to mark the primary reference edges of the mechanical component.
  • Toggle Snapping: Go to View > Snap to Guides to make anchor points lock directly onto your alignment lines.

2. Configure the Paths Tool

Select the Paths tool from the toolbox or press the shortcut key B. In the Tool Options dialog, ensure the following:

  • Set the mode to Design.
  • Check Polygonal if the component consists exclusively of straight lines and sharp corners. This locks each segment to a straight vector and prevents accidental Bézier curve generation. If the part contains both sharp corners and machined arcs, leave Polygonal unchecked.

3. Plot Straight Edges and Sharp Corners

To maintain razor-sharp vertices:

  • Single Click Only: Click once to place an anchor point at a corner, release the mouse button, move to the next vertex, and click again. Never click and drag when placing points on a sharp corner, as dragging pulls out Bézier handles that introduce curvature.
  • Close the Loop: To finish the perimeter, hold Ctrl and click on your initial anchor point. This closes the path without breaking the final corner's angle.

4. Trace Machined Curves and Fillets

Mechanical objects often feature precise rounded corners (fillets) or cylindrical bores:

  • Place an anchor point precisely where the straight edge transitions into the curve (the tangent point).
  • Place the next point where the curve returns to a straight edge.
  • Hold Ctrl, click the straight segment between the two points, and drag outward. This bends the line to match the contour while leaving the adjoining straight segments rigid.
  • Adjust the direction handles to match the radius of the curve.

5. Remove Unwanted Curvature

If you accidentally pull out a handle and curve a mechanical edge that should be flat:

  • Switch the tool mode to Edit (or hold Ctrl while in Design mode).
  • Hold Shift + Ctrl and click directly on the unwanted direction handle. This collapses the handle back into the node, restoring a pure, sharp corner.

6. Convert the Path for Output

Once the path accurately conforms to the mechanical part:

  • For a Selection: Click Selection from Path in the Tool Options dialog, or press Shift + V. This creates a crisp, non-feathered boundary around your part.
  • For Line Art: Go to Edit > Stroke Path, select Solid color, choose your pixel width, and set the cap style to Square or Miter to keep corners crisp and industrial.
