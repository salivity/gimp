GIMP Photocopy Filter: Mimic Toner Spread

This guide explains how to configure the Sharpness and Ramp sliders within GIMP’s Photocopy filter to replicate authentic analog toner spread. By balancing edge feathering and gradient transitions, you can transform crisp digital lines into the slightly bled, imperfect ink typical of physical photocopiers and laser prints.

Understanding Toner Spread

Physical photocopiers work by transferring powdered toner onto paper using static electricity and fusing it with heat. During this process, toner particles scatter slightly beyond line boundaries and bleed into paper fibers. Replicating this digitally requires softening the transition between black ink and white paper while retaining distinct line weight.

Accessing the Filter

  1. Open your image in GIMP.
  2. Navigate to Filters > Artistic > Photocopy... in the top menu.
  3. Ensure the Preview checkbox is enabled to monitor real-time changes.

Adjusting the Sharpness Slider

The Sharpness slider controls how defined or blurry the edges of the ink deposit appear.

  • Lower the Sharpness (Recommended: 0.10 to 0.30): High sharpness produces clean, vector-like lines that look synthetic. To simulate toner scatter, decrease the sharpness value. Lowering this setting creates a slight optical blur along the boundaries of dark regions, mimicking the way micro-particles of toner melt outward under fuser heat.

Adjusting the Ramp Slider

The Ramp slider governs the gradient transition zone between black marks and the surrounding paper background.

  • Lower the Ramp (Recommended: 0.15 to 0.40): A high ramp creates an abrupt cut between black and white, producing a harsh, high-contrast posterization. Lowering the ramp broadens the transition range, giving the ink soft, feathered halftones near the edges. This gradual drop-off provides the visual density gradient characteristic of paper-absorbed toner.

Step-by-Step Settings for Authentic Toner Spread

To achieve a balanced, convincing print effect:

  1. Mask Radius: Set between 5.0 and 15.0. This establishes the local neighborhood contrast for line extraction.
  2. Sharpness: Set between 0.15 and 0.25. This softens edge crispness enough to look printed rather than digital.
  3. Ramp: Set between 0.20 and 0.35. This widens the transition zone to generate toner diffusion.
  4. Percent Black: Adjust between 0.60 and 0.80 to maintain solid core darkness inside dense lines despite the edge softening.
  5. Percent White: Adjust between 0.10 and 0.30 to keep the background clean without clipping the soft edge bleed.

Click OK to apply the filter. For extra realism, apply this filter to a duplicated layer and lower the layer opacity to 85–90% to blend fine line detail back into the composition.
