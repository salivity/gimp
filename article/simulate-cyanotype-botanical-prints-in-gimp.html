Simulate Cyanotype Botanical Prints in GIMP

Simulating an authentic cyanotype botanical print in GIMP involves turning a standard plant photograph into a high-contrast monochrome negative and toning it with the signature Prussian blue palette. This guide provides a step-by-step method to isolate botanical subjects, invert tones to mimic historical sun-print contact sheets, apply accurate cyanotype color gradations, and add subtle paper texture for a realistic, handmade aesthetic.

Step 1: Isolate the Botanical Subject

  1. Open your botanical image in GIMP (File > Open). Images with well-defined leaves, stems, or flowers work best.
  2. Select the botanical element using the Fuzzy Select Tool (U), Select by Color, or the Paths Tool (B) depending on the background complexity.
  3. Invert the selection if necessary (Select > Invert) and delete the background, leaving the plant on a clean, pure white background.

Step 2: Convert to a High-Contrast Monochrome Negative

Traditional cyanotypes are photographic contact prints made using the subject as a silhouette or negative.

  1. Convert the image to black and white by going to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate and choosing Luminance.
  2. Invert the tonal values by navigating to Colors > Invert. The plant should now appear light/white, while the surrounding area turns black.
  3. Adjust the dynamic range via Colors > Levels or Colors > Curves. Increase the contrast so the background is solid black and the internal details of the leaves retain soft gray midtones.

Step 3: Apply the Prussian Blue Gradient Map

The defining characteristic of a cyanotype is its rich, Prussian blue hue ranging from deep navy shadows to pale blue or paper-white highlights.

  1. In the Color Palette, set your Foreground Color to a deep Prussian blue (HTML notation: #0b2545 or #003153).
  2. Set your Background Color to a soft, off-white paper tone (HTML notation: #f4f6f0) or pure white (#ffffff).
  3. Create a custom two-color gradient from Foreground to Background in the Gradients dialog.
  4. With your botanical layer selected, go to Colors > Map > Gradient Map. This automatically maps the dark areas of your negative to Prussian blue and the light areas to the pale highlight color.

Step 4: Add Watercolor Paper Texture

Authentic cyanotypes are coated onto textured watercolor or rag paper.

  1. Create a new layer on top named "Paper Texture" and fill it with a neutral gray or import a seamless watercolor paper texture.
  2. Set the layer blend mode to Multiply or Overlay in the Layers panel.
  3. Lower the opacity to between 30% and 50% so the texture subtly shows through the blue emulsion without overpowering the details.

Step 5: Simulate Hand-Coated Chemical Edges (Optional)

To replicate the look of brushed-on light-sensitive chemicals:

  1. Add a Layer Mask to the cyanotype layer (Layer > Mask > Add Layer Mask).
  2. Select the Paintbrush Tool (P) and choose a rough, textured, or grunge brush.
  3. Paint with black around the outer borders of the image to create uneven, streaky brush edges typical of traditional hand-prepared cyanotype paper.
