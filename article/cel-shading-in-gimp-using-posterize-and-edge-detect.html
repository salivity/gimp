Cel Shading in GIMP Using Posterize and Edge Detect

This guide explains how to convert any photo or digital render into a cartoon, cel-shaded graphic using GIMP. By isolating your image into two separate layers, you will use the Posterize tool to flatten dynamic color gradients into distinct shading steps, while applying edge detection filters to generate crisp, comic-style ink outlines.

Step 1: Prepare the Image Layers

  1. Open your target image in GIMP.
  2. In the Layers panel, right-click the background layer and select Duplicate Layer.
  3. Rename the top layer to "Line Art" and the bottom layer to "Color Base".
  4. Temporarily hide the "Line Art" layer by clicking the eye icon next to it, and select the "Color Base" layer.

Step 2: Smooth the Image (Color Base)

Before posterizing, reducing micro-textures like skin pores or fabric weave ensures clean, cartoon-like blocks of tone.

  1. Navigate to Filters > Blur > Bilateral Filter (or Gaussian Blur with a small radius of 2–4 pixels).
  2. Set the blur high enough to eliminate fine noise while keeping major boundaries distinct. Click OK.

Step 3: Apply the Posterize Tool

  1. With the "Color Base" layer still selected, go to Colors > Posterize.
  2. In the dialog box, lower the Posterize Levels slider (typically between 4 and 8).
  3. Lower values create fewer, flatter tonal bands characteristic of traditional 2D animation, while higher values retain more photographic realism.
  4. Click OK once the lighting transitions appear as solid color shapes.

Step 4: Generate Outlines (Line Art)

  1. Make the top "Line Art" layer visible again and click it to make it active.
  2. Go to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate, choose Luminance, and click OK to make it black and white.
  3. Apply an edge-detection filter via Filters > Edge-Detect > Edge.
  4. Choose an algorithm (such as Sobel), set the Amount to adjust line thickness, and click OK. The image will appear mostly black with white outline edges.
  5. Invert the line colors so the lines are black on a white background by navigating to Colors > Invert.

Step 5: Clean and Enhance the Lines

  1. Go to Colors > Levels.
  2. Drag the black input slider toward the center to darken the lines, and drag the white input slider to the left to turn any light-gray artifacts completely white. Click OK.
  3. (Optional) Run Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur at 0.5 to 1.0 pixel if the extracted edges look too pixelated.

Step 6: Blend the Outlines Over the Colors

  1. Change the Mode of the "Line Art" layer from Normal to Multiply using the dropdown at the top of the Layers panel. This hides the white areas and reveals the posterized colors beneath the black ink lines.
  2. Adjust the Opacity slider of the "Line Art" layer (typically between 80% and 100%) to control the boldness of the final comic outlines.
