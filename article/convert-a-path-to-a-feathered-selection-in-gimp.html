Convert a Path to a Feathered Selection in GIMP

Converting a complex vector path into a feathered selection in GIMP allows you to create soft, blended transitions for intricate cutouts, masks, and composite images. The process involves drawing or activating your vector path in the Paths dialog, transforming that vector data into a standard raster selection boundary, and then applying GIMP's built-in feathering function to soften the edges by a defined pixel radius.

Step 1: Open the Paths Dialog and Choose Your Path

  1. Ensure the Paths dockable dialog is visible. If it is hidden, open it by navigating to Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Paths.
  2. In the Paths list, locate and click on your complex vector path to highlight it.
  3. If the path is not yet created, use the Paths Tool (shortcut: B) to draw your vector outline directly on the canvas.

Step 2: Convert the Vector Path to an Active Selection

You can transform the active vector path into a running selection (the "marching ants" boundary) using one of three methods:

  • Paths Dialog Button: Click the Path to Selection icon (a red square with a dotted border) at the bottom of the Paths dock.
  • Menu Bar: Go to Select > From Path in the top menu.
  • Keyboard Shortcut: Press Shift + V while the path is active.

Your canvas will now display an active selection following the exact contours of your vector path.

Step 3: Feather the Selection Boundary

Vector conversions produce sharp, aliased borders by default. To soften the transition:

  1. Navigate to Select > Feather... from the top menu.
  2. In the Feather Selection dialog, enter the desired blur radius in the Feather selection by field.
    • A radius of 2 to 5 pixels provides subtle anti-aliasing.
    • A radius of 10 to 30+ pixels creates a noticeable, diffused glow or fade.
  3. Choose your measurement unit (pixels are standard for digital work) and click OK.

The selection outline will slightly shift inward or outward, indicating that the edge pixels are now semi-transparent.

Step 4: Preview and Use the Feathered Selection

Because standard selection boundaries do not clearly show the gradient of a feathered edge, you can verify your result using Quick Mask:

  • Press Shift + Q (or click the small square in the bottom-left corner of the image window) to toggle Quick Mask mode. The semi-transparent red overlay will clearly show the gradual transition of the feathered edge.
  • Press Shift + Q again to return to the standard selection view.

You can now invert the selection, apply color adjustments, create a layer mask, or delete content with smooth, non-destructive borders.
