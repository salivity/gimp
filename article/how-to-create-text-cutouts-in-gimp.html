How to Create Text Cutouts in GIMP

Typography cutouts allow you to mask or punch text shapes through solid colors to reveal an underlying photograph, creating a modern, high-impact design. This guide covers the complete step-by-step process in GIMP, from importing your base photo and styling thick typography to using alpha channels and layer masks to produce a clean, dynamic cutout effect.

Step 1: Open Your Base Photograph

  1. Launch GIMP and go to File > Open.
  2. Select the photograph you want visible through the text and click Open.
  3. If prompted to convert the color profile, select Keep or Convert according to your preference.

Step 2: Create a Color Overlay Layer

  1. Go to the Layers panel on the right.
  2. Click the Create a new layer button at the bottom of the panel.
  3. Name the layer "Overlay", set the Fill with option to White (or your chosen background color), and click OK.
  4. Ensure the "Overlay" layer is positioned directly above your photograph in the layer stack.

Step 3: Add and Format Your Text

  1. Select the Text Tool (T) from the Toolbox.
  2. Click on the canvas and type your desired text.
  3. Choose a heavy, bold typeface (such as Impact, Montserrat Black, or Arial Black) so that enough of the underlying photo remains visible through the letterforms.
  4. Scale the font size up using the on-canvas text editor or the Tool Options panel.
  5. Switch to the Move Tool (M) to position your text over the desired focal point of the image below.

Step 4: Cut the Text Out of the Overlay

  1. In the Layers panel, right-click the text layer and select Alpha to Selection. This creates an active selection outline around your letters.
  2. Click the eye icon next to the text layer to hide it.
  3. Select the "Overlay" layer to make it active.
  4. Ensure the layer has an alpha channel by right-clicking the "Overlay" layer and selecting Add Alpha Channel (if grayed out, it already has one).
  5. Press the Delete key (or go to Edit > Clear) to remove the overlay color within the selection boundaries.
  6. Press Ctrl + Shift + A (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + A (macOS) to deselect.

The photograph will now show directly through the transparent letter cutouts on the overlay layer.

Step 5: Add Depth with Shadows

  1. Ensure the "Overlay" layer is selected.
  2. Navigate to Filters > Light and Shadow > Drop Shadow.
  3. Adjust the X and Y offsets and increase the Blur radius to cast a subtle shadow inward onto the photograph, separating the cutout edges from the background.
  4. Click OK to apply the effect.

Step 6: Export the Final Image

  1. Go to File > Export As.
  2. Choose your export format (such as .jpg or .png).
  3. Click Export, set your quality level, and confirm the export.
