GIMP Wind Filter for Speed Lines Guide

The Wind filter in GIMP is a distortion tool that generates directional streaks by displacing pixels along an axis, making it an essential feature for creating dynamic speed lines and action effects. Commonly used in comic illustrations, graphic design, and photo manipulation, this filter simulates high-velocity motion by stretching contrasting pixel edges into linear trails. This guide covers the core function of the Wind filter, its key settings, and how it is utilized to construct convincing speed line effects.

Core Function of the Wind Filter

Located under Filters > Distorts > Wind, the filter analyzes the edges within an active layer or selection and pulls those pixels horizontally across the canvas. When applied to high-contrast elements—such as scattered dots, speckled noise, or line segments—the filter transforms static points into elongated, tapering streaks. These streaks mimic the appearance of objects moving rapidly through space or atmospheric drag.

Essential Settings and Parameters

The Wind filter provides several adjustments to control the appearance and intensity of the generated lines:

  • Style (Wind vs. Blast): The "Wind" option creates smooth, uniform streaks ideal for subtle motion or continuous wind trails. The "Blast" option produces harsher, fractured streaks that resemble explosive motion or intense acceleration.
  • Direction (Left or Right): The filter natively renders streaks horizontally. To create vertical or diagonal speed lines, the layer is typically rotated 90 degrees or to the desired angle before running the filter, then rotated back.
  • Edge (Leading, Trailing, or Both): This setting controls which side of the pixel cluster extends into streaks. Selecting "Leading" stretches the front edge, "Trailing" stretches the back, and "Both" extends the lines in both directions.
  • Threshold: Controls the sensitivity of edge detection. Lower values apply the streaking effect to subtle color variations, while higher values restrict the effect strictly to high-contrast edges.
  • Strength: Determines the length and density of the drawn streaks. Higher values produce longer, more pronounced speed lines.

Creating Speed Lines with the Filter

To generate classic speed lines, artists generally start with a transparent layer containing black and white noise or scattered brush strokes. Applying the Wind filter consecutively stretches these dots into straight horizontal lines.

Once the linear streaks are formed, they can be kept straight for lateral movement or converted into radial "zoom" lines by applying the Polar Coordinates filter (mapping rectangular coordinates to polar). This technique turns horizontal wind streaks into inward- or outward-bursting focus lines, commonly used in manga and action artwork to draw immediate attention to a focal point.
