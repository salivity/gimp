Duotone Print Preparation in GIMP Step-by-Step

Preparing a duotone image for print in GIMP involves converting a standard full-color photograph into a rich, two-tone image mapped to specific inks or spot colors. This process requires stripping existing color information, optimizing tonal contrast for ink saturation, applying a targeted dual-color gradient, and properly formatting the file resolution and color plates for commercial offset or digital printing.

Step 1: Set Up and Convert to Grayscale

  1. Open your image in GIMP by navigating to File > Open.
  2. Strip the standard RGB or CMYK data by going to Image > Mode > Grayscale.
  3. Ensure proper print resolution by selecting Image > Print Size. Set the X resolution and Y resolution to 300 pixels/in (DPI), and verify that the physical dimensions match your target print size.

Step 2: Adjust Image Contrast

Duotone printing relies entirely on tonal values to distribute ink. Deepen your contrast so both highlights and shadows have distinct definition:

  1. Go to Colors > Curves or Colors > Levels.
  2. Increase the contrast slightly by creating an "S-curve" in the Curves tool—pull down the lower quadrant (shadows) and pull up the upper quadrant (highlights).
  3. Ensure highlights are not completely blown out (pure white) unless desired, as paper will show through without ink.

Step 3: Switch Mode to RGB

To apply color tones within GIMP's digital workspace, convert the image back to a color-capable mode:

  1. Go to Image > Mode > RGB.
  2. The image will still appear black and white, but the color channels are now unlocked for duotone mapping.

Step 4: Apply the Duotone via Gradient Map

  1. Open the Gradients dialog via Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Gradients.
  2. Click the Create a new gradient button at the bottom of the dialog.
  3. In the Gradient Editor, set the left endpoint (representing shadows/dark tones) to your primary dark ink color (e.g., Black, Deep Navy, or a specific Pantone-equivalent RGB hex code).
  4. Set the right endpoint (representing highlights/light tones) to your second ink color (e.g., Warm Yellow, Cyan, or Orange) or pure white if paper will act as the base.
  5. Name the gradient and close the editor.
  6. With the new gradient selected as your active gradient, go to Colors > Map > Gradient Map. GIMP maps the shadows to your first color and the highlights to your second color.

Step 5: Fine-Tune Plate Separation

For traditional two-plate offset printing where distinct ink separations are required:

  1. Duplicate your base layer via Layer > Duplicate Layer.
  2. Name one layer for your primary ink (e.g., "Ink 1 - Base") and the other for your secondary ink (e.g., "Ink 2 - Accent").
  3. Use Colors > Curves on each layer independently to isolate which values will be printed by which plate. Pull highlights out of the shadow plate and shadows out of the highlight plate.
  4. If your print shop requires separated grayscale plates, convert each isolated layer to grayscale and export them as individual files labeled with their respective spot color names.

Step 6: Export for Print

  1. Go to File > Export As.
  2. Select *TIFF image (*.tif, .tiff) or PDF from the file format list to preserve image quality without compression artifacts.
  3. In the export settings dialog, ensure compression is set to None or LZW, and keep the color profile embedded.
