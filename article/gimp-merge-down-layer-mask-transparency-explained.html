GIMP Merge Down Layer Mask Transparency Explained

When you merge a layer down onto a layer with an active mask in GIMP, the software combines the visual pixel data while retaining the bottom layer's mask. Rather than discarding the mask or applying it beforehand, GIMP preserves the mask on the newly combined layer, dynamically subjecting the merged upper content to the mask’s existing transparency values.

The Mechanics of the Merge

When executing the Merge Down command where the target (lower) layer contains an active layer mask, GIMP follows a distinct order of operations:

  1. Pixel Blending: GIMP blends the upper layer's RGB pixels directly into the lower layer’s pixel buffer, respecting the upper layer's opacity and blend mode.
  2. Mask Preservation: The lower layer's mask is not discarded or permanently applied to the original pixels. Instead, it remains attached to the resulting merged layer.
  3. Transparency Application: The mask's grayscale data is immediately evaluated against the newly merged pixel data. Any part of the upper layer that falls over a black or gray area of the lower layer's mask becomes concealed or partially transparent.

How Mask Values Affect the Upper Layer

Because the mask remains intact and sits above the combined image data in the rendering pipeline, transparency is incorporated based on the mask's existing grayscale values:

  • White Areas (100% Opacity): Both the upper layer's merged pixels and the lower layer's pixels remain fully visible.
  • Black Areas (0% Opacity): The upper layer's content is completely hidden in these regions, even if the upper layer was fully opaque prior to the merge.
  • Grayscale Areas (Intermediate Opacity): The upper layer’s pixels inherit the exact level of transparency dictated by the gray value of the mask (e.g., 50% gray renders the merged pixels at 50% opacity).

Interaction with Existing Alpha Channels

If the upper layer already contains transparent or semi-transparent pixels, GIMP calculates the final visibility through multiplicative blending:

  • The upper layer's existing alpha channel determines its initial opacity when blending onto the base layer's pixels.
  • The lower layer's mask is then calculated over that result. If a pixel was already at 50% opacity on the upper layer and falls over a 50% gray area on the lower layer's mask, the resulting pixel will have an effective opacity of 25%.

Preserving vs. Baking Mask Data

If your goal is to merge the upper layer into the lower layer without subjecting the new content to the lower layer's mask, you must right-click the lower layer and select Apply Layer Mask before merging down. Doing so bakes the transparency permanently into the bottom layer's alpha channel. Leaving the mask active ensures the mask remains fully editable while governing the entirety of the newly merged composite.
