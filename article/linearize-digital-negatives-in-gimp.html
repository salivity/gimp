Linearize Digital Negatives in GIMP

Creating custom linearization curves in GIMP ensures that digital negatives produce smooth, tonally accurate results when exposed through alternative photographic processes like cyanotype, platinum/palladium, or Van Dyke brown. Because different emulsion chemistries, papers, and ultraviolet light sources compress or expand tonal values non-linearly, calibration is essential. This guide explains the process of printing a standardized step wedge, measuring the resulting print's tonal values, calculating the corrective data, and building an inverse curve in GIMP to apply to all future digital negatives.

Step 1: Print a Target Step Wedge

  1. Create or download a standardized grayscale step tablet containing 21 to 101 discrete gray patches stepping evenly from 0% (pure white) to 100% (solid black).
  2. Invert the step tablet in GIMP (Colors > Invert) to create a negative.
  3. Print the inverted step wedge onto your transparency film (such as Pictorico) using your standard printer settings, resolution, and ink limits. Do not apply any color management profiles or corrections at this stage.

Step 2: Make the Test Print

  1. Contact print the transparency wedge onto your sensitized paper under your UV light source.
  2. Use your standard minimum exposure time to achieve maximum black (Dmax) through the clear film base.
  3. Process, wash, dry, and allow the print to cure completely, as dry-down effects significantly shift optical densities.

Step 3: Measure the Printed Tonal Values

  1. Place the dry print alongside an unexposed, processed sheet of the same paper on a flatbed scanner.
  2. Turn off all automatic exposure, contrast, and color-correction features in your scanning software, and scan the print as a 16-bit grayscale image.
  3. Open the scan in GIMP.
  4. Set the Sample Average tool or the Color Picker Tool to a radius large enough to sample the core of each patch (e.g., 15x15 pixels) without capturing edge artifacts or paper texture.
  5. In the Color Picker or Sample Points dialog, set the readout mode to Pixel or Percentage.
  6. Record the measured grayscale value for each patch, starting from the unprinted paper white (0% target) up to the darkest printed step (100% target).

Step 4: Calculate the Correction Points

To compensate for the chemical response, you must map the target values to the values needed to produce them.

  1. List your intended values (Input) alongside the measured values (Output). For example, if the 50% target step measured at 70% gray in your physical print, your print is printing too dark in the midtones.
  2. Reverse the relationship: to get an actual print value of 50%, you need an input value from the step that printed as 50%. If the 35% negative patch produced a 50% print value, the correction must map 50% to 35%.
  3. Compile these coordinates into an Input/Output list for each major step along the tonal scale (typically 0%, 10%, 20%... 100%).

Step 5: Construct the Curve in GIMP

  1. Open GIMP and navigate to Colors > Curves.
  2. Set the Channel to Value.
  3. Set the Curve type to Smooth for continuous gradients, or Freehand if you require precise segment adjustments.
  4. Click along the diagonal line to create control points matching your calibration steps.
  5. Select each point and enter the corresponding values into the Input and Output fields located at the bottom of the Curves dialog.
  6. Check that the endpoints remain anchored: Input 0 should map to pure white (or the threshold of paper base), and Input 255 (or 100%) should map to your maximum printing density.

Step 6: Save and Apply the Curve

  1. In the Curves dialog, click the Manage Presets button (the small icon next to the Presets dropdown) and choose Save Current Settings to Preset.
  2. Name the preset according to your specific process (e.g., Cyanotype_Fabriano_PtWare).
  3. To prepare any positive digital image for printing:
    • Convert the image to grayscale (Image > Mode > Grayscale).
    • Invert the image to create a negative (Colors > Invert).
    • Open Colors > Curves, select your saved preset from the dropdown, and click OK.
    • Flip the image horizontally (Image > Transform > Flip Horizontally) so the emulsion side of the film directly contacts the paper emulsion during exposure.
