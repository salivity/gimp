How to Create an Inverted Adjustment Layer in GIMP

This guide explains how to construct a non-destructive, inverted adjustment layer stack in GIMP using layer groups, blend modes, and inverted layer masks. Because GIMP does not feature dedicated adjustment layer tools out of the box, combining these features allows you to target specific areas of your image with precise color, exposure, and tonal adjustments while leaving the underlying data intact.

1. Set Up the Layer Group

  1. Open your image in GIMP.
  2. In the Layers panel, click the Create a new layer group icon at the bottom.
  3. Name this group "Adjustment Group".
  4. Ensure the Layer Group's mode is set to Normal (or Pass-through depending on whether you want the blend effects contained solely within the group).

2. Add the Adjustment Layer

  1. Duplicate your base image layer by selecting it and clicking the Duplicate layer icon (or pressing Shift + Ctrl + D).
  2. Drag this duplicated layer into your "Adjustment Group".
  3. Apply your desired changes directly to this layer using the Colors menu (such as Curves, Levels, Hue-Saturation, or Exposure).
  4. Alternatively, you can create a solid color or gradient layer inside the group and set its blend mode to Overlay, Multiply, or Soft Light to alter tone and contrast.

3. Add an Inverted Layer Mask to the Group

Adding a mask directly to the group controls the visibility of the entire adjustment stack at once.

  1. Right-click the "Adjustment Group" in the Layers panel and select Add Layer Mask.
  2. In the dialog box, select Black (full transparency) to hide the adjustments completely from the start, or select White (full opacity). Click Add.
  3. If you selected White, invert it immediately by clicking the mask thumbnail in the Layers panel, then navigating to Layer > Mask > Invert (or pressing Ctrl + I). The mask thumbnail will turn black, concealing all adjustments inside the group.

4. Paint to Reveal the Adjustments

  1. Select the Paintbrush Tool (P) from the Toolbox.
  2. Set your foreground color to White (#FFFFFF).
  3. Click on the black layer mask thumbnail next to the layer group to ensure the mask is active.
  4. Paint over the areas of your canvas where you want the adjustments to appear. Painting with white reveals the adjustments; painting with black hides them again.
  5. Lower the brush Opacity or adjust its Hardness for smoother transitions and subtle blending.

5. Expand the Stack

To add multiple adjustments under the same inverted mask:

  • Add additional layers inside the "Adjustment Group" above the first adjustment layer.
  • Adjust their individual opacities and blend modes.
  • The single inverted group mask will continue to dictate where all contained adjustments are visible across the canvas.
