Using GEGL Spherize for 3D Sphere Textures in GIMP

The GEGL Spherize filter in GIMP is a specialized geometric distortion tool designed to wrap flat, two-dimensional surfaces around a simulated three-dimensional spherical volume. This article explains the technical purpose of the Spherize operation, how it counteracts texture stretching, and why it is an essential step for generating convincing spherical projections, planets, and rounded 3D assets directly within a 2D image editor.

Transforming Planar Coordinates to Spherical Geometry

Standard digital textures exist in a flat, Cartesian coordinate system (X and Y planes). When a flat texture is viewed on a rounded surface, perspective laws dictate that the center of the surface faces the viewer directly, while the edges recede into the distance at steep angles.

The GEGL Spherize operation applies a non-linear warp that pushes pixels outward from the center while progressively compressing pixels toward the boundary of a defined circular radius. This mathematical transformation mimics spherical trigonometry, converting planar maps into an orthographic projection of a hemisphere.

Eliminating Texture Distortion and Pinching

Mapping an unprepared flat texture directly onto a 3D sphere often leads to unnatural linear distortion, pole pinching, and edge misalignment. By pre-processing textures with GEGL Spherize, you achieve several critical visual corrections:

  • Foreshortening: Details near the perimeter of the sphere compress naturally, simulating the curvature of an object turning away from the camera.
  • Central Expansion: Features in the direct center appear magnified and flat relative to the viewer, replicating normal optical focal behavior.
  • Seamless Masking: Spherize creates a clean circular boundary that makes isolating the sphere from its background simple using circular selections or alpha masks.

Key GEGL Spherize Parameters for Texture Work

To achieve physically accurate results, the GEGL engine provides specific controls:

  • Radial vs. Horizontal/Vertical Mode: Allows users to spherize uniformly in all directions (standard for complete 3D spheres) or along a single axis (useful for cylindrical objects).
  • Angle of View: Dictates the simulated lens field of view. Lower angles produce a telephoto, flatter sphere appearance, while higher angles yield a wide-angle, exaggerated fisheye bulge.
  • Curvature / Amount: Controls the depth of the simulated protrusion, ranging from subtle surface convexity to full hemispherical bulging.
  • Interpolation Methods: Uses high-precision resampling algorithms (such as Lanczos or Cubic) to prevent pixelation and aliasing as detail density increases near the edges.

Common Use Cases in 2D and 3D Workflows

The primary utility of the GEGL Spherize filter spans both direct 2D rendering and 3D asset preparation:

  • Procedural Planets and Celestial Bodies: Turning noise patterns (like Perlin or Simplex noise) into realistic planetary surfaces with authentic limb darkening and horizon curvature.
  • Normal and Bump Map Preparation: Curving surface relief textures so that lighting engines calculate realistic specular highlights on spherical geometry.
  • UI and Game Asset Design: Quick creation of glossy buttons, orbs, lenses, and biological elements such as irises and pupils.
