How to Stabilize Line Art with Smooth Stroke in GIMP

Creating clean digital line art with a mouse or graphics tablet can be difficult due to natural hand tremors and jittery brush behavior. GIMP resolves this issue with its built-in Smooth Stroke tool option, which dynamically averages mouse movements to produce smooth, flowing curves. This guide details how to locate the Smooth Stroke feature and configure its core settings—Quality and Weight—to achieve crisp, professional line work without unwanted distortion or hardware lag.

Enabling Smooth Stroke

Smooth Stroke is available for GIMP's primary painting tools, including the Paintbrush, Pencil, and Airbrush.

  1. Select your preferred drawing tool (such as the Paintbrush, shortcut P) from the Toolbox.
  2. Open the Tool Options dockable dialog (if not visible, go to Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Tool Options).
  3. Scroll down to find the Smooth stroke checkbox and toggle it on.

Once enabled, two primary parameters become active: Quality and Weight.

Essential Settings for Stabilization

1. Weight

The Weight slider controls the strength of the stabilization effect. It dictates how heavily GIMP smooths the path between the cursor and the rendered stroke, functioning similarly to an elastic leash or stroke stabilizer in other digital painting software.

  • Low Values (10–20): Best for small details, tight corners, and quick cross-hatching. This introduces minimal cursor drag while removing micro-jitters.
  • Medium Values (30–50): Ideal for general inking and line art. It produces clean, continuous outlines without making the cursor feel unresponsive.
  • High Values (60–100+): Best for long, sweeping curves, architectural arcs, or large borders. The stroke will lag noticeably behind your cursor to enforce maximum smoothness.

2. Quality

The Quality slider determines the mathematical precision and sample density GIMP uses to calculate the curve.

  • Higher values produce mathematically cleaner, rounder curves by taking more computational samples.
  • Extremely high values can cause input lag on complex brushes or lower-end hardware.
  • A value between 20 and 50 offers the ideal balance of smooth trajectory calculations without introducing stroke latency.

Recommended Workflow

For optimal results, adjust the Weight depending on the specific element you are drawing rather than leaving it at a fixed number. Use a higher weight for the outer silhouette of a character or object, then lower the weight to ink internal details like eyes, clothing folds, or texture lines.
