Create Custom Gradient Maps in GIMP for Color Grading

Gradient mapping is a powerful technique in GIMP that replaces the luminance values of an image with specific colors from a gradient, allowing you to achieve cinematic, stylized, or vintage color grading. By mapping dark tones to shadow colors and light tones to highlight colors, you gain full control over the mood and aesthetic of your photos. This guide walks you through designing a custom color gradient, applying it to your image as a gradient map, and refining the results using blend modes and opacity.

Step 1: Open the Gradients Dialog

  1. Launch GIMP and open the image you want to grade.
  2. Go to Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Gradients to open the Gradients panel.
  3. Right-click anywhere inside the Gradients list and select New Gradient to open the Gradient Editor.

Step 2: Design Your Custom Gradient

In a gradient map, the leftmost point represents the darkest shadows, the center represents midtones, and the rightmost point represents the brightest highlights.

  1. Set Shadow Color: Right-click the left endpoint of the gradient slider, select Left Endpoint's Color, and pick a deep, rich tone (such as dark navy, teal, or deep burgundy).
  2. Set Highlight Color: Right-click the right endpoint, select Right Endpoint's Color, and choose a soft, light tone (such as warm cream, pastel yellow, or soft peach).
  3. Add Midtones (Optional): Right-click the segment and select Split Segment at Midpoint. Click on the newly created middle point, right-click, and assign a complementary midtone color.
  4. Rename your gradient at the top of the editor and close the editor dialog; the gradient will save automatically.

Step 3: Duplicate the Image Layer

  1. Go to the Layers panel.
  2. Select your base image layer and click Layer > Duplicate Layer (or press Ctrl+Shift+D / Cmd+Shift+D).
  3. Name this duplicate layer "Color Grade."

Step 4: Apply the Gradient Map

  1. In the Gradients dockable dialog, ensure your newly created gradient is actively selected.
  2. Select the duplicate "Color Grade" layer in the Layers panel.
  3. Navigate to Colors > Map > Gradient Map. GIMP will immediately replace the black-and-white tonal range of that layer with the colors of your active gradient.

Step 5: Blend for a Subtle, Artistic Look

A raw gradient map is often too intense, so blending it with the original image creates a professional finish:

  • Change the Blend Mode: In the Layers panel, set the mode of the "Color Grade" layer to Soft Light, Overlay, or Color.
    • Soft Light gives a subtle contrast boost with gentle color tinting.
    • Overlay delivers a punchier, high-contrast look.
    • Color applies the hues without altering the brightness values.
  • Adjust Opacity: Lower the layer opacity to somewhere between 20% and 50% to naturally integrate the grade with the original photo.
  • Use a Layer Mask (Optional): Add a layer mask (Layer > Mask > Add Layer Mask) to brush away the color grade from specific areas, such as skin tones or backgrounds.
