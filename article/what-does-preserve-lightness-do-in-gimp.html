What Does Preserve Lightness Do in GIMP?

The "Preserve Lightness" checkbox in GIMP's Color Balance tool ensures that the overall brightness of your image remains stable while you adjust its color tones. By locking the luminance of the pixels, this feature allows you to alter color casts across shadows, midtones, and highlights without unintentionally darkening or overexposing the image.

How Color Balance Affects Brightness

In digital imaging, colors carry different perceived brightness levels. The human eye perceives green and yellow as significantly brighter than blue and red. Consequently, when you adjust color balance sliders:

  • Pushing sliders toward cyan, magenta, or yellow alters the amount of light represented in those color channels.
  • Adding warmer tones like yellow can artificially brighten an area, while boosting blue can make it noticeably darker.

Unchecked vs. Checked

When Unchecked: Adjusting the sliders changes both the color hue and the overall luminosity of the selected tonal range. Increasing red, green, or blue will raise the total channel values, causing the image to become brighter and potentially blowing out highlights or muddying midtones.

When Checked: GIMP calculates the perceived luminance of the original pixels and adjusts the RGB values proportionally. The tool modifies the hue and saturation while dynamically compensating the exposure so that the perceived brightness stays nearly identical to the original image.

When to Use It

  • Keep it enabled: For standard color correction, fixing white balance issues, and creative color grading where you only want to shift the mood or temperature of an image without altering the exposure.
  • Disable it: When you deliberately want a color shift to affect brightness—such as adding blue to simulate a darker, nighttime mood, or boosting warm tones to create a bright, sunlit glow.
