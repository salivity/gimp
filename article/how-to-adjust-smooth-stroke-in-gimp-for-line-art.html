How to Adjust Smooth Stroke in GIMP for Line Art

This guide explains how to fine-tune the Smooth Stroke feature in GIMP to achieve steady, clean line art without unwanted input lag. By balancing the Weight and Quality settings located in the Paint tool options, digital artists can control the damping effect and real-time responsiveness of their stylus, eliminating jitter while maintaining exact control over fine details.

Enabling Smooth Stroke

  1. Select any freehand drawing tool from the Toolbox, such as the Paintbrush (P), Pencil (N), or Ink tool.
  2. Open the Tool Options panel (if closed, open it via Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Tool Options).
  3. Scroll down and check the box labeled Smooth stroke to reveal its adjustment sliders: Quality and Weight.

Adjusting Damping with the Weight Setting

The Weight slider functions as the damping control. It determines how heavily the algorithm stabilizes your stroke by simulating physical drag or a "leash" between your cursor and the actual drawn mark.

  • Lower Weight (10–25): Maximizes responsiveness. The brush closely follows your stylus with minimal latency, making it ideal for quick hatching, fast gestures, and sharp corners. However, it provides minimal jitter reduction.
  • Moderate Weight (30–60): The optimal range for most high-precision line art. It filters out hand tremors and micro-stutters while keeping the brush responsive enough to follow intentional curves accurately.
  • High Weight (70–100): Maximizes stabilization. The stroke visibly lags behind the cursor, pulling the line smooth. Use this for sweeping, broad outlines or long geometric curves where speed is secondary to stability.

Adjusting Precision with the Quality Setting

The Quality slider controls the computational resolution and mathematical precision of the smoothing algorithm.

  • Low Quality (10–30): Uses fewer calculation steps to interpolate the curve. This improves rendering performance on low-end hardware, but can introduce subtle angular artifacts or flattened arcs during slow movements.
  • High Quality (50–90): Calculates intermediate points more densely, ensuring that curved strokes remain mathematically round and true to your intended arc without clipping corners. For high-resolution line art (300 DPI or higher), keep this value above 50.

Recommended Calibration Workflow

  1. Set Quality to 60. This provides a reliable computational baseline without introducing performance bottlenecks.
  2. Set Weight to 0, then draw a controlled circle or S-curve at your natural drawing speed to observe baseline hand jitter.
  3. Increase Weight in increments of 10 until hand tremors disappear while maintaining an acceptable level of cursor tracking speed.
  4. If the brush begins to skip sharp corners, decrease the Weight slightly rather than lowering the Quality.
