GEGL Lens Distortion for Planet Panoramas in GIMP

The GEGL Lens Distortion filter in GIMP is a powerful tool used to transform standard wide-angle and 360-degree panoramic photos into circular, miniature planet projections. This article explains the primary role of the filter in the tiny planet creation workflow, how it manipulates geometric perspective to warp rectangular images into spherical shapes, and which settings are essential for achieving a clean, seamless result.

The Role of Lens Distortion in Tiny Planet Projections

The fundamental purpose of the GEGL Lens Distortion filter in creating miniature planets is to simulate extreme optical curvature. While GIMP includes traditional polar coordinate mapping, the GEGL Lens Distortion filter offers precise control over spherical bulging, barrel distortion, and edge displacement. By applying heavy barrel distortion to a prepared panoramic image, the filter bends the horizontal horizon line into a 360-degree closed loop, creating the illusion of a self-contained, round world.

Key Distortion Parameters and Their Functions

To successfully generate a miniature planet effect, several key sliders within the GEGL Lens Distortion dialogue must be adjusted:

  • Main Distortion: This setting controls the primary spherical curve. Increasing the value creates intense barrel distortion, pushing the center of the image outward while pulling the outer edges together until the horizon wraps into a circle.
  • Edge Distortion: This controls curvature specifically around the outer boundaries of the canvas. Adjusting this parameter ensures that the ground or sky blends smoothly without harsh geometric pinching at the corners.
  • Zoom: Warping a rectangular canvas into a sphere often introduces empty corners or pushes the planet outside the visible frame. The zoom parameter rescales the transformed image directly inside the filter, allowing the entire planet to fit neatly within the canvas boundary.
  • Brighten and Shift Adjustments: These controls help correct optical falloff and realign the center of distortion if the focal point of the panorama needs re-centering.

Workflow Integration

In a typical tiny planet workflow, the panorama is first resized to a 1:1 square aspect ratio and flipped vertically (so that the ground is at the top if mapping outward). Applying the GEGL Lens Distortion filter at this stage wraps the top edge into the outer circumference and compresses the sky into the center of the sphere, or vice versa.

The non-destructive preview feature of the GEGL engine allows users to observe the wrapping process in real time, making it easy to fine-tune the curvature until the seam where the left and right edges meet becomes invisible.
