How to Create a Thermal Heat Camera Effect in GIMP

This guide provides a straightforward method for turning standard photographs into realistic thermal heat camera visuals using GIMP. By converting your base image into a high-contrast grayscale map and applying gradient mapping that emulates temperature spectrums, you can accurately replicate the infrared look produced by specialized thermal imaging devices.

Step 1: Prepare the Image and Duplicate the Layer

  1. Open your image in GIMP by navigating to File > Open.
  2. Go to the Layers panel on the right.
  3. Right-click your background layer and select Duplicate Layer to preserve your original image.

Step 2: Convert to High-Contrast Grayscale

Thermal maps rely on tonal brightness values to determine fake temperatures (with bright areas acting as "hot" zones and dark areas as "cold" zones).

  1. Select the duplicated layer.
  2. Go to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate and click OK.
  3. Go to Colors > Levels or Colors > Curves.
  4. Increase the contrast significantly: pull the black point slider inward to darken shadows and pull the white point slider inward to brighten highlights. This ensures a broad separation between cold and hot temperatures.

Step 3: Select or Create a Thermal Gradient

  1. Open the Gradients dockable dialog (Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Gradients).
  2. Look for existing thermal-style gradients, such as Full saturation spectrum CW, Incandescent, or Firecode.
  3. To build a custom FLIR-style gradient:
    • Right-click in the Gradients dialog and select New Gradient.
    • Set the color stops from left (cold) to right (hot) in this sequence: Black > Dark Blue > Magenta/Purple > Red > Orange > Yellow > White.
  4. Make this gradient your active gradient by clicking it in the gradient palette.

Step 4: Apply the Gradient Map

  1. With your high-contrast grayscale layer selected, go to Colors > Map > Gradient Map.
  2. GIMP will instantly replace the grayscale tones with the colors from your active gradient, mapping black pixels to the cold colors and white pixels to the hot colors.

Step 5: Add Heat Bloom and Glow

  1. Duplicate your colored thermal layer.
  2. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur. Set the blur radius to around 10–20 pixels depending on your image resolution, then click OK.
  3. In the Layers panel, change the blend mode of the blurred layer to Screen or Addition.
  4. Lower the layer opacity to between 30% and 50% to create a soft infrared radiation glow around hot objects.

Step 6: Add Digital Noise and Sharpening

  1. Create a new transparent layer on top of all layers (Layer > New Layer).
  2. Go to Edit > Fill with FG Color (fill it with 50% neutral gray).
  3. Go to Filters > Noise > CIE lch Noise or RGB Noise to add slight digital sensor grain.
  4. Set the layer blend mode to Overlay or Soft Light and lower the opacity to around 15–25% to mimic low-resolution digital thermal sensors.
