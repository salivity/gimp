What Is Selective Gaussian Blur in GIMP?

The GEGL Selective Gaussian Blur filter in GIMP is an edge-preserving blurring tool designed to smooth flat areas and reduce image noise while keeping sharp borders and distinct details intact. Unlike standard blur algorithms that blend all neighboring pixels indiscriminately, this filter evaluates the contrast between pixels before applying the blur. It is widely used for portrait retouching, surface smoothing, and noise reduction because it allows editors to soften textures without losing crisp outlines.

How It Works

A traditional Gaussian blur operates by calculating a weighted average of all surrounding pixels within a defined radius, which inevitably softens hard edges and causes blurring across boundaries. The Selective Gaussian Blur modifies this process by introducing a contrast threshold.

When analyzing surrounding pixels, the filter checks the difference in luminosity and color values against this threshold. If the difference between adjacent pixels is greater than the set limit, the filter treats that transition as an edge and excludes those pixels from the blur calculation.

Key Parameters

The filter relies on two primary settings to balance smoothing and detail preservation:

  • Radius: Determines the spatial size of the blur area in pixels. A larger radius increases the spread of the smoothing effect across uniform areas.
  • Max. Delta: Defines the maximum allowable difference in pixel values for blurring to occur. A lower delta strictly limits the blur to nearly identical tones (preserving almost all edges), while a higher delta allows the blur to bridge stronger contrasts, behaving more like a standard blur.

Primary Use Cases

  • Skin Smoothing: Digital retouching frequently uses this filter to soften skin blemishes, pores, and uneven tones while leaving eyelashes, lips, hair, and facial contours completely sharp.
  • Noise and Artifact Reduction: It efficiently smooths out high-ISO sensor noise or JPEG compression artifacts in solid backgrounds (such as clear skies or studio backdrops) without degrading subject outlines.
  • Surface Cleanup in Graphic Design: Illustrators and designers use it to flatten minor gradients and inconsistencies in scanned line art or digital paintings while maintaining defined linework.
