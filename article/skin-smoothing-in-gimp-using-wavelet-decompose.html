Skin Smoothing in GIMP Using Wavelet Decompose

Multi-frequency skin smoothing using GIMP's Wavelet Decompose filter is a professional retouching technique that separates an image into detail layers (high frequencies) and color/tone layers (low frequencies). By isolating fine pores, medium blemishes, and overall skin tones onto separate layers, you can smooth uneven tones and remove imperfections without blurring or destroying the natural skin texture.

Step 1: Open the Wavelet Decompose Filter

  1. Open your portrait in GIMP.
  2. Duplicate the base image layer to preserve your original work.
  3. With the duplicated layer selected, go to the top menu and select Filters > Generic > Wavelet Decompose.
  4. In the dialog box, set the Scales slider between 5 and 7 (a value of 5 is standard for most portraits).
  5. Check the option to Create a layer group to keep your workspace organized, then click OK.

Step 2: Understand the Layer Structure

The filter generates a new layer group containing several layers set to the Grain merge blending mode, along with one base layer:

  • Wavelet Residual: The bottom layer in the group containing broad colors, shadows, and low-frequency tones without sharp details.
  • Scale 1 to Scale 2: High-frequency layers containing micro-textures, such as fine pores, stray hairs, and sharp wrinkles.
  • Scale 3 to Scale 5 (or higher): Medium-frequency layers containing skin blotchiness, larger pimples, and subtle shading transitions.

Step 3: Smooth Skin Tones (Low Frequencies)

  1. Select the Wavelet Residual layer.
  2. Choose the Smudge Tool or a soft Airbrush with a low opacity (around 15–30%).
  3. Gently blend uneven skin patches, redness, and harsh shadows.
  4. Alternatively, use the Free Select Tool to select an area of skin, add a slight feather, and apply Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur. Because texture is preserved on the upper layers, the skin will not look plastic or out of focus.

Step 4: Remove Blemishes and Spots (Medium Frequencies)

  1. Identify the layer where the specific blemish is visible by toggling layer visibility on Scale 3, Scale 4, or Scale 5.
  2. Select the Healing Tool or the Clone Tool.
  3. Set the tool mode to Normal and set the source sampling to Current Layer.
  4. Sample clean grey areas on that specific scale layer and paint over dark or bright spots representing acne, blotches, or unwanted shadows.

Step 5: Clean Up Fine Texture (High Frequencies)

  1. Select Scale 1 or Scale 2 to target sharp imperfections, such as flaking skin, scratches, or individual prominent pores.
  2. Use the Healing Tool with a small brush size to remove the defects.
  3. If the overall skin texture looks too harsh across the entire image, select Scale 1 or Scale 2 and slightly lower the layer opacity (e.g., to 70–80%) or apply a minimal Gaussian Blur (0.5 to 1.0 px) directly to that layer.

Step 6: Review and Finalize

  1. Zoom out to view the entire portrait at 100% scale.
  2. Toggle the visibility of the entire Wavelet Decompose layer group on and off to compare your retouching against the original image.
  3. If the smoothing effect is too intense, reduce the overall Opacity of the Wavelet Decompose group to blend the retouched result naturally with the original layer beneath it.
