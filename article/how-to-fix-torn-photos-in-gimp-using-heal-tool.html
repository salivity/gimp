How to Fix Torn Photos in GIMP Using Heal Tool

Restoring damaged vintage photographs is straightforward with GIMP's Heal tool, a powerful feature designed to blend textures while preserving underlying lighting and color values. This guide explains the step-by-step process of preparing a scanned image, configuring the Heal tool, selecting optimal sample points, and seamlessly mending severe cracks and physical tears to return historic prints to their original appearance.

1. Prepare the Workspace

Open your scanned image in GIMP. Vintage scans should ideally be at least 300 to 600 DPI to retain sufficient texture detail. Before making any edits, go to the Layers panel, right-click the background layer, and select Duplicate Layer (or press Shift + Ctrl + D). Working on a duplicated layer ensures all edits are non-destructive and allows you to compare your progress against the original scan.

2. Select and Configure the Heal Tool

Activate the Heal tool from the Toolbox or press the keyboard shortcut H. In the Tool Options panel, apply the following settings:

  • Brush: Choose a standard round brush with soft edges (such as Hardness 050 or Hardness 075). Soft edges ensure seamless transitions between repaired and untouched areas.
  • Size: Adjust the brush size so it is slightly wider than the crack or tear you are fixing. A brush that is too large will introduce unwanted blur into surrounding details.
  • Alignment: Set alignment to Aligned if you plan to paint along long tears, or None if you prefer repeatedly sampling from a single source area.
  • Sample Merged: Check this option if you are applying repairs onto a separate transparent layer above the original photograph.

3. Set the Source Sample Point

The Heal tool works by taking pixel texture from an undamaged source area and blending it into the damaged destination.

  1. Identify a clean, undamaged area of the photograph directly adjacent to the tear that shares the same grain, tone, and focus.
  2. Hold down the Ctrl key (or Cmd on macOS) and click on the clean area to establish your sample point.

4. Paint Out the Cracks and Tears

Zoom in close to the damage (Z or Ctrl + Mouse Wheel). Click and drag along the line of the crack using short, deliberate strokes rather than long continuous drags.

Because vintage photo paper has natural grain, long strokes can cause repetitive patterns. Constantly reset your sample source point (Ctrl + Click) as you move across the photograph, ensuring that the source region always matches the local lighting, perspective, and surface texture of the area you are repairing.

5. Managing Complex Edges and High-Contrast Tears

Deep physical tears often cross distinct borders, such as the edge of a coat against a light background.

  • Use Selections: Use the Free Select Tool (Lasso) to isolate one side of an edge before healing. This prevents the Heal tool from pulling dark pixels into light areas or blurring distinct structural boundaries.
  • Combine with the Clone Tool: For missing chunks of paper where structure is entirely lost, use the Clone Tool (C) first to rebuild the lost lines or edges, then lightly paint over the seam with the Heal Tool to blend the photographic grain smoothly.
