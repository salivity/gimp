How to Remove Motion Blur in GIMP

Accidentally shaking your camera can ruin an otherwise great photograph, but you can restore clarity using the free photo-editing software GIMP. This guide covers the most effective methods to reduce motion blur, recover lost edge detail, and improve image sharpness using built-in sharpening filters and high-pass frequency separation techniques.

Method 1: The Sharpen (Unsharp Mask) Tool

The Unsharp Mask is the quickest native tool in GIMP to counter minor motion blur by increasing contrast along the edges of the subject.

  1. Open the Image: Launch GIMP and open your blurred photo via File > Open.
  2. Duplicate the Layer: In the Layers panel on the right, right-click your image layer and select Duplicate Layer (or press Ctrl+Shift+D / Cmd+Shift+D). This allows you to work non-destructively.
  3. Apply the Filter: With the duplicated layer selected, go to the top menu and select Filters > Enhance > Sharpen (Unsharp Mask).
  4. Adjust the Settings:
    • Radius: Determines the width of the edges being sharpened. For motion blur, set this between 1.0 and 3.0.
    • Amount: Controls the strength of the sharpening effect. Increase this gradually (e.g., between 0.5 and 1.5) until details become clearer without introducing excessive grain.
    • Threshold: Helps prevent sharpening smooth areas like skies or skin. Keep this low (between 0.0 and 0.05).
  5. Apply and Blend: Click OK. If the effect is too aggressive, lower the Opacity of the sharpened layer in the Layers panel to blend it naturally with the original.

Method 2: The High Pass Filter Overlay

The High Pass method gives you precise control over edge enhancement without affecting the overall color or exposure of the photograph.

  1. Duplicate the Original Layer: Right-click the background layer and choose Duplicate Layer.
  2. Apply High Pass: With the top layer active, navigate to Filters > Enhance > High Pass.
  3. Set the Filter Radius: Adjust the Std. Dev. (Standard Deviation) slider until you can faintly see the outlines and details of your blurred subject against a gray background. A value between 1.0 and 4.0 works best for motion blur. Click OK.
  4. Change the Layer Mode: At the top of the Layers panel, open the Mode dropdown menu (currently set to "Normal") and change it to Overlay, Soft Light, or Hard Light.
    • Soft Light: Delivers a subtle deblurring effect.
    • Overlay: Delivers a balanced, standard sharpening effect.
    • Hard Light: Delivers a strong effect for heavier blur.
  5. Fine-Tune the Result: Reduce the layer's Opacity to eliminate any harsh halos or visual artifacts.

Tips for Best Results with Shaken Photos

  • Mask Problem Areas: If sharpening introduces noise into smooth backgrounds, add a Layer Mask (Layer > Mask > Add Layer Mask) to your sharpened layer and paint over the background with a black brush to confine the sharpening solely to the blurred subject.
  • Avoid Over-Sharpening: Excessive sharpening creates white outlines (halos) around edges and amplifies digital noise. Always zoom in to 100% view to monitor artifact generation while adjusting sliders.
