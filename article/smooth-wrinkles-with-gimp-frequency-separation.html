Smooth Wrinkles with GIMP Frequency Separation

Frequency separation is the industry-standard retouching technique that divides an image into two distinct layers: low frequency (colors, shadows, and skin tones) and high frequency (fine lines, pores, and skin texture). By isolating these elements in GIMP, you can soften the harsh shadows and discoloration of wrinkles on the tone layer while preserving authentic pore structure on the texture layer, preventing the artificial, plastic look caused by standard blurring.

Step 1: Set Up the Frequency Separation Layers

  1. Open your portrait in GIMP.
  2. Duplicate the background layer twice:
    • Rename the lower duplicate to Low Frequency.
    • Rename the upper duplicate to High Frequency.

Step 2: Create the Low Frequency Layer

  1. Hide the High Frequency layer by clicking the eye icon next to it.
  2. Select the Low Frequency layer.
  3. Go to Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur.
  4. Adjust the Size X and Size Y sliders until the fine skin texture and pores disappear, but the broader shapes, shadows, and skin tones remain recognizable (typically between 5 and 10 pixels depending on image resolution).
  5. Click OK.

Step 3: Extract the High Frequency Layer

  1. Unhide and select the High Frequency layer.
  2. Go to Image > Apply Image (or use the traditional layer subtraction method):
    • If using GIMP's native tools: Set the High Frequency layer's mode to Grain Extract. Right-click the layer stack and select New from Visible. Name this new layer High Frequency Texture, delete the temporary layer, and place it above Low Frequency.
    • Change the blending mode of your final High Frequency layer to Grain Merge.
  3. Verify the setup: When both the High and Low frequency layers are visible, your portrait should look identical to the original unprocessed image.

(Note: GIMP 2.10+ also offers an automated version of this via Filters > Enhance > Wavelet Decompose, which splits the image into detail scales and a residual color layer automatically.)

Step 4: Smooth the Wrinkle Tones (Low Frequency)

Wrinkles appear prominent primarily because of the contrast between deep shadows inside the crease and highlights along the ridge.

  1. Select the Low Frequency layer.
  2. Choose the Clone Tool or Healing Tool from the Toolbox:
    • Set the brush to a large, soft-edged round brush.
    • Set the tool opacity between 30% and 50% for gradual blending.
    • Ensure the tool source is set to Current Layer.
  3. Sample smooth, adjacent skin tones and gently paint over the dark shadow lines of the wrinkles.
  4. Alternatively, use the Free Select Tool (Lasso) with a high feather radius (e.g., 20–40 px) to outline the wrinkled area, then apply a mild Gaussian Blur to blend the tones seamlessly.

Step 5: Clean Up Harsh Creases (High Frequency)

  1. Select the High Frequency layer.
  2. Select the Healing Tool or Clone Tool:
    • Use a small, soft-edged brush roughly the width of the wrinkle's fine crease.
    • Set the tool source to Current Layer.
  3. Hold Ctrl and click to sample clean skin texture directly adjacent to the wrinkle.
  4. Paint directly over the deep crack or line of the wrinkle.

Because you are working strictly on the texture layer, this removes the sharp physical crack in the skin without smudging or distorting the underlying color and lighting.
