How to Adjust Output Levels in GIMP for Newsprint

Porous paper stocks like newsprint absorb ink rapidly, causing a phenomenon known as dot gain where ink spreads and darkens the printed image. To prevent shadow details from plugging into solid, muddy black blobs and to avoid wet ink bleeding through the paper, prepress designers compress an image's dynamic range. This guide explains how to use the Output Levels control in GIMP to restrict maximum black density and safely prepare images for newsprint reproduction.

Why Output Levels Matter for Newsprint

Standard digital images utilize a full tonal range from 0 (pure black) to 255 (pure white). On high-gloss coated stock, 100% black ink dries cleanly on the surface. On uncoated, absorbent newsprint, a 100% black tone causes the ink to bleed beyond its halftone grid, obscuring surrounding details.

By adjusting the Output Levels instead of the Input Levels, you compress the overall dynamic range rather than remapping or clipping it. Raising the black output level maps pure black (0) to a dark gray, capping the maximum ink density to a threshold the paper can physically hold without bleeding.

Step-by-Step: Compressing Contrast with Output Levels in GIMP

  1. Open Your Image in GIMP
    Load the image you intend to prepare for print. If you are printing in black and white, ensure your image is converted to grayscale by going to Image > Mode > Grayscale.

  2. Open the Levels Tool
    Navigate to the top menu and select Colors > Levels....

  3. Locate the Output Levels Section
    Look at the bottom of the Levels dialog box. Beneath the main histogram, you will see a gradient bar labeled Output Levels with two values:

    • Left slider/box (Black point): Defaults to 0.
    • Right slider/box (White point): Defaults to 255.

  4. Raise the Black Output Level
    To prevent ink saturation in the shadows:

    • Increase the left Output Level value from 0 to a higher number, typically between 15 and 25 (corresponding to roughly an 85% to 90% maximum black limit).
    • As you raise this value, you will see the deepest blacks in your preview turn into a dark charcoal gray. This prevents the press from laying down heavy, bleeding layers of black ink.

  5. Adjust the White Output Level (Optional)
    Newsprint paper is naturally off-white or grayish. If your highlights appear too stark against the dull paper tone, lower the right Output Level slider from 255 down to around 245–250. This slightly compresses the highlight side of the tonal curve.

  6. Compensate Midtones with Input Levels
    Compressing the output range can occasionally make an image appear flat or washed out on a bright computer screen.

    • Look back up at the Input Levels histogram.
    • Drag the middle slider (Gamma) slightly to the left (e.g., between 1.05 and 1.15) to open up the midtones, ensuring faces and central subjects remain visible once absorbed by the paper.

  7. Apply the Changes
    Click OK to commit the adjustments to your layer.

Recommended Newsprint Target Values

  • Maximum Ink Density: A black output level set to 20 limits black coverage to approximately 92%, which is the standard safety limit for most standard coldset web offset newspaper presses.
  • Highlight Threshold: Setting the white output level to 250 prevents extreme loss of detail in pale skies or light skin tones.
