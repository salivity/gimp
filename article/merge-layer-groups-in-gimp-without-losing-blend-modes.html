Merge Layer Groups in GIMP Without Losing Blend Modes

Merging a layer group in GIMP often alters the final image because blend modes like Screen, Multiply, or Overlay behave differently when isolated from underlying layers. This guide explains how to merge down a layer group while preserving the exact composite appearance of all internal blend modes using GIMP's group blend modes and the "New from Visible" function.

Why Layer Groups Change Appearance When Merged

By default, layer groups in GIMP 2.10 and later are set to Pass-through mode. In this mode, layers inside the group blend directly with layers located beneath the group in the layer stack.

When you use Merge Layer Group, GIMP isolates the group contents and blends them only against each other over an empty transparent background before flattening. This strips away the backdrop that blend modes like Multiply or Screen need to display properly.

Method 1: Merging the Group Down into the Layer Below

If your goal is to merge the layer group directly into the layer beneath it:

  1. Verify Group Blend Mode: Select the Layer Group. In the Layers panel, verify that the Mode dropdown is set to Pass-through so the layers inside blend seamlessly with the backdrop.
  2. Select the Layer Group: Click on the group folder in the layer stack.
  3. Merge Down: Go to Layer > Merge Down (or right-click the group in the Layers panel and select Merge Down).

GIMP will render the internal blend modes against the layer immediately below the group and combine them into a single, flattened layer.

Method 2: Merging the Group Itself into a Single Layer

If you want to collapse the group into a single layer without merging it into the layers below, you must bake the composite appearance accurately:

  1. Set Group Mode to Normal: If the layers inside the group are only meant to blend with each other (and not with layers outside the group), change the group’s Mode from Pass-through to Normal. This ensures the internal composite preview matches the final merged output.
  2. Merge Layer Group: Right-click the group folder and select Merge Layer Group.

Note: If the layers inside the group require an underlying background to maintain their appearance (for instance, a semi-transparent light beam set to Screen), you cannot merge the group into a standalone transparent layer without changing its appearance. In that case, use Method 3.

Method 3: The "New from Visible" Technique (Foolproof Method)

When complex blend modes interact across multiple layers, this method guarantees 100% visual fidelity:

  1. Isolate the Desired Output:
    • Hide any layers you do not want included in the merge by clicking the eye icon next to them in the Layers panel.
    • Ensure the layer group and the background/underlying layers it interacts with are visible.
  2. Create New from Visible:
    • Right-click anywhere in the Layers panel and choose New from Visible (or go to Layer > New from Visible).
    • GIMP creates a new, flattened layer at the top of your layer stack that matches your exact canvas appearance.
  3. Clean Up:
    • Delete or hide the original layer group and the original lower layers that were baked into the new layer.
    • Re-enable the visibility of any unrelated layers you hid in Step 1.
