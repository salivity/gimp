GIMP Unsharp Mask: Radius vs Threshold Explained

GIMP’s Unsharp Mask filter is one of the most effective tools for enhancing image sharpness by increasing local contrast along edges. While the Amount slider dictates the strength of this enhancement, the Radius and Threshold sliders control where and how the effect is applied. Understanding the difference between Radius and Threshold is essential for bringing out crisp details in your photos without introducing distracting halos or amplifying digital noise.

The Purpose of the Radius Slider

The Radius slider determines the width of the edge area affected by the sharpening calculation. Unsharp Mask works by blurring a copy of the original image to detect edges; the Radius defines the pixel width of this blur.

  • Small Radius (0.1 – 1.5 pixels): Ideal for fine textures, intricate patterns, and standard-resolution web images. It sharpens small details like individual hairs, fabric weaves, and eyelashes.
  • Large Radius (2.0+ pixels): Better suited for high-resolution photographs or images with broad, sweeping details. It creates broader contrast shifts along major boundaries.
  • The Risk: Setting the Radius too high creates visible, unnatural bright and dark halos around objects, making the photo look over-processed.

The Purpose of the Threshold Slider

The Threshold slider acts as a selective filter that determines how different adjacent pixel values must be before the sharpening effect is applied to them.

  • Threshold of 0: Sharpening is applied uniformly across the entire image, including smooth gradients and subtle textures.
  • Higher Threshold (1 – 10+): Protects smooth areas—such as skin tones, clear skies, or out-of-focus backgrounds—from being sharpened. Sharpening is restricted only to distinct, high-contrast edges.
  • The Risk: Setting the Threshold too high will completely prevent subtle details from being sharpened, leading to uneven or patchy results where only the most extreme edges are enhanced.

How Radius and Threshold Work Together

Radius controls the spread of the sharpening effect, while Threshold controls the selectivity.

To achieve optimal results:

  1. Adjust the Radius first according to the size of the details you want to enhance and the overall resolution of your image.
  2. Increase the Threshold just enough to suppress noise in flat or smooth areas without losing sharpness on the primary subjects.
