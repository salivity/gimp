Measure Compass Bearings on Topo Maps in GIMP

This guide explains how to calculate precise compass bearings between two points on a scanned topographical map using GIMP. By utilizing the built-in Measure Tool's multi-point angle feature, you can bypass manual protractor math and display a direct azimuth readout relative to grid north directly within the software interface.

Step 1: Align the Scanned Map to Grid North

For any digital bearing measurement to be accurate, the map's north-south grid lines (or neatlines) must align perfectly with GIMP's vertical canvas axis.

  1. Open your scanned map in GIMP.
  2. Drag a vertical guide from the left ruler across a known north-south grid line.
  3. If the grid line deviates from the vertical guide, select the Rotate Tool (Shift + R).
  4. Click on the image and adjust the angle until the grid line matches the vertical guide exactly, then click Rotate.

Step 2: Configure the Measure Tool

  1. Select the Measure Tool from the Toolbox or press Shift + M.
  2. In the Tool Options dock on the left, ensure the units are set to your preferred readout (pixels, inches, or millimeters for distance; angles are always displayed in degrees).
  3. Ensure the Use Info Window option is checked if you prefer a floating window with coordinate and angle readouts, or refer to the status bar at the bottom of the canvas.

Step 3: Establish the North Reference Line

A standard compass bearing is measured clockwise starting from North (0° / 360°). GIMP’s default readout measures angles against the horizontal axis. To get a direct compass bearing, create a two-legged angle where the first leg acts as the North reference.

  1. Zoom in closely to your starting position (Point A).
  2. Click on Point A and drag the cursor straight upward toward the top of the canvas (Grid North).
  3. Hold Ctrl while dragging to constrain the line to strict 15-degree increments, locking it directly onto the vertical 90° axis.
  4. Release the mouse button. You now have a reference line pointing due North.

Step 4: Measure the Bearing to the Destination

  1. Hover over the starting point (Point A) until the cursor changes to indicate an angle vertex.
  2. Hold Shift, click on Point A, and drag the second measurement line to your destination (Point B).
  3. Release the mouse button.

Step 5: Read the Bearing

Look at the GIMP status bar at the bottom of the window or the Measure Info Window.

  • GIMP will display the length of both legs and the Angle between them.
  • If your destination is to the east (clockwise), the angle readout represents your direct Grid Bearing (0° to 180°).
  • If your destination lies west of North, GIMP will still measure the interior angle between the two lines. To convert this into a standard 360° azimuth, subtract the displayed angle from 360° (for example, a reading of 45° to the west equals a bearing of 315°).

Step 6: Adjust for Magnetic Declination

The readout obtained from the grid line represents your Grid Azimuth. To convert this measurement for field use with a magnetic compass:

  1. Locate the declination diagram in the map's margin.
  2. Add or subtract the specified Grid-to-Magnetic (G-M) angle depending on whether magnetic north lies east or west of grid north in that specific map quadrant.
