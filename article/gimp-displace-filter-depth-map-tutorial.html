GIMP Displace Filter Depth Map Tutorial

This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on using the Displace filter in GIMP to create realistic depth map effects. You will learn how to generate a custom grayscale depth map from an existing image, apply displacement to wrap text, patterns, or graphics around complex surfaces, and blend the final result for a natural, three-dimensional look.

1. Prepare Your Base Image

Open your primary image in GIMP by navigating to File > Open. This should be the surface you want your graphic to conform to, such as fabric, skin, or a textured wall.

2. Generate the Depth Map

The Displace filter uses brightness values to determine distortion: white areas shift the pixels in one direction, black areas shift them in the opposite direction, and 50% neutral gray remains unaffected.

  1. Right-click the base layer in the Layers panel and select Duplicate Layer.
  2. Rename this duplicate layer to Depth Map.
  3. Convert the layer to grayscale by going to Colors > Desaturate > Desaturate and selecting Luminance.
  4. Increase the contrast to make the depth distinctions clearer by going to Colors > Levels and dragging the black and white point sliders toward the center.
  5. Soften harsh transitions by applying a slight blur via Filters > Blur > Gaussian Blur. A radius of 2 to 5 pixels typically prevents jagged pixel edges during displacement.
  6. Hide this layer by clicking the eye icon next to it in the Layers panel.

3. Add the Overlay Graphic

  1. Create a new transparent layer or paste the graphic, texture, or text you want to map onto the surface.
  2. Position and scale the graphic over the desired area of the base image.
  3. If using text, right-click the text layer and select Discard Text Information to convert it into a standard raster layer.

4. Apply the Displace Filter

  1. Select your graphic layer in the Layers panel.
  2. Navigate to Filters > Map > Displace... to open the filter settings.
  3. In the Displace dialog, set the Displacement Mode to Cartesian.
  4. Set both the X displacement and Y displacement source inputs to your hidden Depth Map layer.
  5. Adjust the X/Y displacement shift values. Start with small values (typically between 5 and 20 pixels) depending on the resolution of your image. Higher values create stronger distortion along the contours of the depth map.
  6. Click OK to apply the displacement.

5. Blend the Final Image

To integrate the displaced graphic naturally into the base surface:

  1. Change the graphic layer's Blend Mode in the Layers panel from Normal to Multiply, Overlay, or Soft Light.
  2. Lower the layer's Opacity to between 70% and 90% to allow the lighting of the underlying image to show through.
